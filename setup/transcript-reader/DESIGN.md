# Transcript reader - design checkpoint

Status: phase 1 prototype, 2026-10-17. Decisions answered 2026-10-21 (see the end); the overall shape is still being tried on chosen sessions before the real build.

## What was built

A throwaway prototype in `prototype/`: `build_prototype.py` (Python standard library, no model, no network) reads the Claude Code session logs of the main home and every registered second mate, plus each home's fleet records, and fills `template.html`.
Run `python3 setup/transcript-reader/prototype/build_prototype.py` from the repo root, then open `setup/transcript-reader/out/prototype.html`.
The page embeds private transcript text, so `out/` is gitignored. `--stats` prints counts only.

- **Conversation** (view A): one session read back in order, in a 27rem column (phone width). Wake-ups fold to a one-line label, and three or more in a row collapse to one line. Every other turn gets `Needs you` or `No action` from fixed word rules. A `Needs you` reply shows its asking lines first (the ask box) when the reply is clipped, and highlighted in the text otherwise; only an ask you have not written after stays `Needs you` (see "Asks that mean what they say"). A reply over 90 words shows its first lines and a "Show full reply" button. Buttons and `j`/`k`/`n` jump between your messages and between asks. A Firstmate instruction shows the steering message it delivered, not the pointer line.
- **Fleet timeline** (view B): one time-ordered list across you, the main firstmate, the second mates and their workers. Each row shows who to whom. Phone width has no room for seven swim lanes. The rows are update lines and steering messages from the fleet records, plus your messages and the homes' replies, which only the transcripts have. A row opens its full text and, when found, the turn that handled it. A "Waiting on you" panel comes from each home's own summary.

## What the real data showed

Corpus: 30 home sessions, 2026-09-17 to 2026-10-17, 3,030 turns. Fleet view: the last 72 hours.

- **Folding.** 82% of turns are wake-ups. Folding the ones that ask nothing hides 72% of turns but only 36% of the words. The 12% of wake-ups that do ask (305, mostly after a worker update) hold another 23% of the words.
- **Runs.** Folded wake-ups come in 422 runs: median 2 turns, one run in ten 12 or more, the longest 143.
- **Messages sent while busy.** 75 of your 360 messages (21%) arrived while the agent was mid-turn. They sit inside the running turn, not at a turn start. A parser that splits only at turn starts misses one message in five. The prototype splits there.
- **Rule reliability**, hand-checked blind (labels written before seeing the rule's verdict). The first 40 replies agreed 78%. The misses were negated asks, reminders of open items and promises about later, and the rules were tuned on them. A fresh 40 then agreed 90%: all 20 wake-ups, 16 of 20 replies to you. The 4 errors were 3 false alarms (optional offers, a bold heading phrased as a question, a bold label) and 1 miss (a polite instruction). The heading, label and instruction cases are fixed. Optional offers still count as asks, which errs toward showing too much.
- **Reminders.** 662 replies only restate items already waiting on you. The prototype marks them `No action` with a small "reminds you of open items" tag.
- **Spine.** In 72 hours: 146 update lines (112 routine), 3 steering messages, 23 of your messages, 32 replies. Your messages and the replies exist only in transcripts. Update lines and steering messages exist only in fleet records, and cleanup deletes them when a task closes. Only 25 of 146 update lines could be tied to the turn that handled them. Routine updates often wake nobody, and some landed while no loaded session was running.
- **Open items.** Folding update keys gives 58 decisions never marked settled. 56 of them are older than three days, and many were settled some other way. The homes' own summaries list 49 decisions waiting on you. 12 of the 19 with a recorded age are over a week old, and 5 of 6 homes flag their books as out of step with live state.

## Decisions for the captain

1. **Wake-ups that ask.** Fold every wake-up, or keep the 12% that ask open? Recommended: keep them open. Folding all would have hidden 305 asks in this month of sessions.
2. **Reminders.** Does "still waiting on you" count as `Needs you`? Recommended: no. Mark it `No action` with the reminder tag, and keep the open list in the fleet view.
3. **How much of a kept reply to show.** Recommended: the asking lines first, then the first lines of the reply, with the rest behind "Show full reply" once it passes about 90 words.
4. **Timeline spine.** Recommended: fleet records plus your messages and the homes' replies, with the handling turn one tap away. Raw turns never become the default rows.
5. **Source of "waiting on you".** Recommended: each home's own summary only. Do not derive open items from update keys.
6. **Scope and delivery.** Recommended: the main home and the second mates, not workers' own sessions. Default window of 3 days, with a session picker for older reads. Rebuild with one command, and serve it with the reader's own server (`setup/transcript-reader/serve.sh`, port 8768) so it opens in a browser tab (see README for the supported browsers). No auto-refresh in the first version.

## Answers (2026-10-21)

1. Keep wake-ups that ask open.
2. Reminders are tagged, not `Needs you`.
3. Ask-first-then-clip is the right layout. How asks get picked out (today: the word rules above) is reopened once the app's overall shape is settled.
4. to 6. As recommended.

To try the shape on particular sessions, keep a reading list with `python3 setup/transcript-reader/reader.py` (`add`, `remove`, `list`, `panes`; no argument rebuilds every listed session). A session can be named by id, by its first 8 characters, or by its Herdr pane label. Only listed sessions are read, each under the home that holds its working folder, and the fleet timeline starts at the earliest of them. The session picker shows each session by its Herdr name (pane or tab label, then the pane title), and the address keeps the chosen session across reloads.

## Answer sheet prototype (2026-10-21)

A grill-me review reply asks for keyed answers (`Reply with: A1=...; A2=...`). `prototype/build_questionnaire.py` finds those replies with fixed rules: a card table with `ID` and `Answer` columns (choices in backticks), a `**A2 revised.**` question followed by backtick choice bullets, a checkpoint table of answers already sent, and `**B1:**` cards under a "locked" heading. `questionnaire.html` lets the choices be picked or written, then copies one line such as `A1=accept;A2=inline`. Only a choice the reply itself calls a recommendation is marked one; none is guessed. The reader links each such reply to the sheet. Three layouts were tried (all cards, one at a time, compact); the captain picked all cards, so only that one remains.

Viewing beside a Herdr tab in VS Code: `workbench.browser.newTabPlacement: "sideGroup"` opens VS Code's Integrated Browser (1.142) in a reused, locked group beside the terminal editor. One key can show and hide that group without closing the page or asking for an address: while a browser tab exists (`browserEditorOpen`), it focuses the first group (Herdr) and toggles it maximized, which hides the browser group and keeps its page; with no browser tab it opens the reader. `runCommands` and `workbench.action.browser.open` go in `terminal.integrated.commandsToSkipShell` so the key works while the Herdr terminal has focus.

```json
{ "key": "ctrl+alt+r", "command": "runCommands", "when": "browserEditorOpen",
  "args": { "commands": ["workbench.action.focusFirstEditorGroup", "workbench.action.toggleMaximizeEditorGroup"] } },
{ "key": "ctrl+alt+r", "command": "workbench.action.browser.open", "when": "!browserEditorOpen",
  "args": "http://localhost:8768/setup/transcript-reader/out/prototype.html" }
```

## Send and follow (2026-10-21)

Like Lavish's "comment and send back to the agent", without an agent blocking on a poll: the served page sends a reply or a passage comment straight into the session's Herdr pane (`herdr agent prompt`, no model involved), and follows the pane Herdr has focused. The captain chose:

1. A focused session that is not on the reading list is added automatically.
2. Send goes straight to the pane, with the exact text visible before sending. No confirm step. (For one comment, "Sends exactly" shows it; for a batch, see below.)

The message points at the reply instead of copying it (its time and first words, plus a quote of at most 100 characters for a passage), because the session already holds the text. Sending, following and refreshing need the page served over http by the reader's own server (`setup/transcript-reader/serve.sh`), so `reader.py` now prints the served links first; the server rebuilds the pages when a listed log changes, which replaces the `file://` auto-reload. The pages under `out/` are now served only through the guarded routes in `api.js`, so the shared static handler's wildcard CORS header no longer exposes them. How it works and what it touches: `README.md`.

**Batches and the exact text (owner's decision, 2026-10-26).** Several comments can be collected and sent as one numbered message, with what is in the box last. That message is longer than the box and the pointer line, so it is not shown in full by default: "Exact text" shows it on a click. To keep the preview from going unnoticed, the bar shows a one-line note while the batch is not empty ("3 comments - Exact text shows what Send types"). No confirm step is added, and Send's routing is unchanged. In "Sends exactly", a pointer longer than 90 characters (a reply the session no longer holds adds a log path and part of the reply) shows as one line that opens on a tap, followed by the words as written; the text sent is the same. The batch list itself starts folded on every screen (owner's request, 2026-10-30), so a batch does not take the conversation's room; the heading shows the count and opens it. Add leaves it as the reader left it, and a switch of session or an emptied batch folds it again. This matches README.md.

Not verified end to end: no message was sent to a real pane while building this (the tests fake Herdr), and the VS Code shortcut above was not tried in the captain's window.

## Codex crew panes (2026-11-02)

The local reader now supports native Codex CLI rollouts alongside Claude Code logs.
Codex writes rollout JSONL under `~/.codex/sessions/YYYY/MM/DD/rollout-*.jsonl`.
The first `session_meta` record supplies the root session id, working directory and `source: "cli"`.
The reader accepts only that native root source, so guardian and other subagent rollouts do not become pane conversations.

Herdr's Codex pane snapshot does not reliably provide the rollout id, so discovery uses read-only `pane process-info`.
The reader requires a foreground Codex process and maps its exact working directory to the newest native root rollout for that directory.
This same mapping is used by the API's state, Sync, and Send paths.
If the foreground is another Codex rollout or is not Codex, Send stops before clearing keys or prompting the pane.

The Codex parser translates `response_item` messages into the existing exchange model.
It reads `input_text`, `output_text`, custom tool calls and custom tool results, plus plaintext reasoning summaries when present.
It ignores `event_msg` duplicates and encrypted reasoning content.
Codex tool inputs use the existing sanitized tool-step path, so command output remains separate from the conversation payload and sensitive paths keep their existing redaction rules.

Codex skill references begin with `$` and open an autocomplete popup.
Send waits 1.2 seconds before clearing the composer and calling `herdr agent prompt` so that popup cannot capture the message.
The local API tests use fake Herdr panes and throwaway Codex rollout metadata; no real crew pane receives a test message.
Hosted and phone copies are not expanded by this change.

## Copilot CLI panes, read only (2026-11-05)

The captain asked for a pane that runs GitHub's Copilot CLI (`cp-ontology-learning`) to read in the reader like a Claude or Codex one.
Herdr already reports such a pane as `agent: "copilot"`, but with no session id, and `api.js` only listed Claude and Codex panes.
This ship reads a Copilot conversation; it never sends to one (see "Send" below).

**The files.** Copilot keeps `~/.copilot/session-state/<session id>/events.jsonl` for each session: one JSON record per line, `{type, data, id, timestamp, parentId}`, first record `session.start` (its `data.context.cwd` is the working folder). `PMOS_READER_COPILOT_HOME` (Python) and `copilotHomeDir` (`api.js`) name another home for tests and labs. The records the parser reads, and nothing else:

| Record | Becomes |
|---|---|
| `user.message` | A turn's message: `data.content`, what was typed. Never `transformedContent`, which wraps it in the date and any skill's text. `delivery: "steering"` marks one sent while the agent was busy, which starts an absorbed turn as for Claude. The record's `messageId` is the turn id, so pins hold. |
| `assistant.message` | `content` is a working note when a tool call follows it in the turn and the reply when none does. `reasoningText` (the plaintext summary; `reasoningOpaque` and `encryptedContent` are never read) is a working note only when a tool call follows it; the summary before the final answer is dropped, so it never joins the reply. A summary's bold heading line loses its asterisks (notes are drawn as plain text). `toolRequests` make the steps. |
| `tool.execution_start`, `tool.execution_complete` | A step for a call no message requested (the `copilot_*` tools are logged only here), and every call's result. A call that failed, or a shell call with a nonzero `shellExecution.exitCode`, is a failed step. |
| `session.compaction_complete` | The same "Earlier context was summarised" divider Claude's compaction leaves. The turn that was running goes on after it. |

Anything with a top-level `agentId` (a helper agent's own prompt and replies) or a `parentToolCallId` stays out: the conversation holds the `task` call that ran it and its result. Hooks, usage, the system prompt, permission and model records are never looked at.

**Tool steps.** The parser renames each Copilot call to the Claude tool it is drawn as (`bash` to Bash, `view` to Read, `rg` and `glob` to Grep and Glob, `web_fetch`, `skill`, `task`, `ask_user` and so on; `COPILOT_TOOLS` in `build_prototype.py`) so `tool_steps.py` makes the step, its summary, its failed-output tail and its dotfile and credential redaction exactly as it does for Claude. The step sheet still names the tool as Copilot's log has it (`n`). `apply_patch` carries its whole patch as one string; `tool_steps.patch_blocks` turns each file in it into a Write or MultiEdit (one edit per hunk) or a Delete, so the page draws the same diffs. Redaction is Claude's: a call whose path (for a patch, its source or its `*** Move to:` target; for a search, any of its `paths` or its `glob`) is a dotfile or a credential path keeps the row and none of its text. Tools the reader has no mapping for (the browser and Playwright tools, MCP tools) are "Tool" steps named as the log has them.

**Which session a pane shows.** `agent_session` is absent for a Copilot pane, so the reader asks `herdr pane process-info` for the pane's foreground and takes the `copilot` process's pid. Where Codex is matched by working folder, that cannot work here: the folder holds every Copilot session ever started in it, and a `/resume` shows an older one while the process stays the same. What the process does leave behind:

- Its own log, `~/.copilot/logs/process-<start ms>-<pid>.log`, gets `<time> [INFO] Registering foreground session: <id>` when the process starts on a session and again at each `/resume` and `/new`. The newest such line is the pane's session. Checked on the live pane: the process started on an empty new session, `/resume` moved it to `75c5495c`, and the pane title and conversation were that one's.
- **That log is rotated at 10 MB** (`...<pid>.1.log`) and some builds fill it with thousands of lines a minute, so the line can be in the rotated file or be gone within minutes. The reader therefore reads every file of the process (only what each gained since the last look, and from the start when a rotation put another file at the name: its inode changed), keeps the newest line any look saw, and writes it to `out/copilot-panes.json` under `<start>-<pid>`. `api.js` and `build_prototype.py` both read and write that record, so a later look in either, with the logs rotated away, still knows. Entries older than 14 days are dropped.
- `session-state/<id>/inuse.<pid>.lock` names the session the process *started* on, so it is the last resort, and only for a session that has an `events.jsonl`: here it would name the empty session the process opened before the `/resume`.
- Otherwise the pane maps to nothing. It is never guessed from a label, a title or a working folder.

Limit, stated plainly: a pane whose process was resumed and never looked at while its log still held the line, and whose log has since been rotated twice, shows no conversation until the next `/resume` or `/new` writes a new line. The reader's server looks every 2 seconds while a page is open, so this needs a page closed for roughly the time a spammy log takes to fill 20 MB.

**Session ids.** Every Claude and Codex log is named for its session, so `path.stem` was the id. A Copilot conversation is `events.jsonl` inside the folder named for the session, so `build_prototype.log_key` and `log_session_id` (and `canonicalSessionId` in `api.js`) take the folder's name; the parsed-log cache is named by it, and `reader.py add`, `follow`, `adopt` and the reading list take a Copilot id, a prefix or the events path as they do any other. A Copilot session never joins a Claude `/clear` chain, and `pane_neighbours` leaves its log out of one.

**Send.** The reader must never send to a Copilot pane. The page's message bar says "Sending to Copilot is not supported yet. Copy still works." first in its chain of reasons, on every copy (served, hosted, offline), in plain text rather than the fault colour, and the Send button is off. If a request gets through anyway (a stale page, the hosted copy's queue, a `curl`), `sendPrompt` and the hosted queue route refuse with the same sentence (409, code `copilot_send_unsupported`) as their first act after the reading-list check: the session's log is looked up on disk, so Herdr is not asked anything and no key or text is sent. A sealed queued request cannot be read on the way in, so `drain.js` hits the same refusal when it sends it and writes it back as a refused receipt. Tests: `api.test.js` ("Copilot: ..."), `copilot.test.js`.

What Send for Copilot would take (not built): Herdr's `agent prompt` as the transport, but its text box has to be known: whether Ctrl+K and Ctrl+U empty it (Claude's box does; Codex needed a 1.2 s pause for its `$` popup, and Copilot has `/` and `@` popups of its own), whether Enter submits a pasted multi-line message, how a message sent mid-turn is queued (`delivery: "steering"` suggests Copilot has a notion of it), and what Herdr's `blocked` state means for its permission prompts. `checkForeground` would use the pid-to-session mapping above. All of it has to be tried on a throwaway Copilot pane, never a real one.

**Not changed.** Claude and Codex behaviour, and the parsed-log cache of their logs. The hosted and phone copies were not expanded: a Copilot session on the reading list builds into them as any conversation does (checked with `sync.py --build-only --session <id>`: its object carries `agent: "copilot"`, so the shell's message bar says the same), but the default scope of `sync.py` (fleet homes, `~/workspace`, live panes) lists Claude logs only, so a Copilot session reaches the hosted copy only through `--reading-list` or `--session`. Taking Copilot sessions into the default scope would mean listing `session-state/*/events.jsonl` in `sync.py`'s scope walk and the pane test it applies.

## Claude under another config folder (2026-11-05)

The captain focused the tab `cp-ontology-search` with Follow on, and the reader loaded nothing.
That pane runs Claude Code with `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR=~/.claude-personal`, a second account.

**Cause (read-only: Herdr's snapshot, `pane process-info`, the two config folders).** Herdr reported `agent: claude` for the pane but no `agent_session`, because its Claude hook lives in `~/.claude/settings.json` only.
The reader's fallback for a pane with no session is the process check in `checkSessions`: the pane's foreground pid looked up in the running processes' records.
It read only `~/.claude/sessions`, and the log lookup read only `~/.claude/projects`, so the pane's pid had no record, its session id stayed null, and Follow had nothing to add.
The record was in `~/.claude-personal/sessions/<pid>.json` (`pid`, `sessionId`, `cwd`), and the log in `~/.claude-personal/projects/<folder>/<id>.jsonl`, the same layout as `~/.claude`.

**The change.** Every place that read one Claude folder reads the folders of all config folders, `~/.claude`'s first:

- **Which folders.** `otherClaudeHomes` (`claude-homes.js`, re-exported by `api.js`; the Herdr organizer's adapter uses it too) and `other_claude_homes` (`build_prototype.py`) answer the same: each `~/.claude*` folder that holds a `projects/` or `sessions/` folder, the folder `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` names for the reader's own process, and each folder of `PMOS_READER_CLAUDE_HOMES` (absolute paths, separated like `PATH`).
  No account name is hard-coded.
  A plain file (`~/.claude.json`), another tool's folder with neither child (`~/.claude-mem`) and a folder of another name that nothing names are left out.
  Why this rule and not "the folder of the running process's environment": it also finds the logs of sessions whose process has ended, which a reading list keeps, and it needs no `ps eww` of other processes; the environment of a live process (`ps eww -p <pid>` does show `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` on macOS) is the better proof of which folder a pane uses, and stays available as a fallback if an account ever lives outside both `~/.claude*` and `PMOS_READER_CLAUDE_HOMES`.
  The answer is looked up again at each use (Python reuses it for the one second the list of logs is reused), so an account set up while the server runs is found without a restart.
- **Running processes.** `runningClaude` (`api.js`) and `running_claude_sessions` read `sessions/*.json` of every folder into one pid-to-session map, so `checkSessions`, `pane_session` and Send's foreground check work unchanged for such a pane: a Claude pane with no session id takes the session of its foreground process, and a pid with a record in no folder gets none (never guessed from a label, a folder or a cwd).
- **Logs.** `logPath`, `log_index` (hence `all_logs`, `find_session_file`, the `/clear` chains and the fleet timeline), `home_sessions`, `reader.session_files` (`reader.py add`, `follow`, `adopt`, `resolve`) and `message_link.session_log` read every folder's `projects/`.
  A session id is looked up in the usual folder first, so a copy of a log in a second folder never shadows it.
  The server's watch needed no change: it follows `logPath`, so a new turn written under the second folder rebuilds the page like any other.
- **Left alone on purpose.** `PMOS_READER_PROJECTS` and `create({ projectsDir, claudeSessionsDir })`, which a test or a measurement uses so it does not touch the real logs, still mean exactly those folders: no other folder is added when either is given (`create` takes `claudeHomes` to name the extra ones in a test).
  Claude, Codex and Copilot panes read as before.

**Send.** Sending to such a pane follows the existing Claude checks unchanged (a live pane, a foreground whose record, in whichever folder, names this very session).
It was tested with a fake Herdr only; nothing was sent to a real pane.

**Tried (2026-11-05).** Read-only against the live tab `cp-ontology-search` (pane `wJP:p2`, session `bfda597d`): `reader.py panes` listed it after the change and not before; a lab copy on a spare port with Follow on, Herdr focused on that tab, added the session within one poll and showed its first turn and the reply.
Live updating was shown on an invented session under a second config folder with a name nothing here knows (`~/.claude-second` in a fake home, a stand-in `herdr` that answers the read commands and refuses all others): with the page open, each turn appended to its log was on the page within the 9 seconds waited, twice, without a reload ("1 turn", then "2 turns", then "3 turns").
`python3 -m unittest` and the Node suites pass; `test_claude_homes.py` and the "Claude homes" tests in `api.test.js` cover the folder rule, the pane mapping, the logs, the rebuild on a newer log and Send's checks.

**Not done.** Safari or a phone; the hosted copy's own build beyond what falls out (`sync.py --session` resolves such a session by id through the same lookup, and its default scope now lists the logs of every folder); a Claude config folder outside both `~/.claude*` and `PMOS_READER_CLAUDE_HOMES` (name it in that variable); anything for Copilot's or Codex's own config folders.

## A more reliable answer sheet (2026-10-21)

The herdr_fix session's Oct 21 13:55 review got no answer sheet: its choices were written `choice: a / b / c` without backticks. Rather than add one more special case, the card reader now works in layers. Every keyed table row, bold question lead or asked-about keyed bullet is a card. Its choices come from the first rule that finds some: backtick values, lettered `a)` choices (also inside the question), a labelled or slash-separated list of short choices, an option table under a "G2 explained" heading, or a short recommendation such as `accept`. The reply line's example answer and any answer it allows for every card (`needs explanation`, `defer`) are added. A card with none of these is still shown and answered by writing one. A choice is marked recommended when the recommendation opens with it, names its letter, or (added in the answer-sheet fixes below) names it as the only choice anywhere in its text; naming two choices marks none. On two weeks of real replies that ask for keyed answers, sheets found went from 27 to 52 and cards from 103 to 225 (201 with choices). The 13 replies still without a sheet are reminders of an earlier sheet or use other key styles (`FX=`, `P, S, Y = confirm`).

The sheet also no longer reloads itself after every rebuild, which had closed "What this is about" mid-read. It takes the new data in place, redraws only when the open questionnaire changed, keeps the scroll position, and remembers which "What this is about" sections are open.



## Answer-sheet fixes (2026-11-02)

From the sbds design review (S1 to S6), each with its test in `sheet.test.js` or `test_questionnaire.py`.

- **Fill recommended** fills every open card that has a starred choice. It filled 1 of 3 on the lab review because only a recommendation that opened with its choice was starred ("The **export** date" was not). The button says what it does and is off when no open card has a starred choice.
- **The preview is what Send types.** The line shows the pointer and the picked answers, character for character, with no `A1=?` gaps; the cards it leaves out are named in muted text under it ("B1 and B2 are left out of what Send types."), not in red.
- **A questionnaire the session answered shows as answered.** `sent` (the keyed line the owner wrote after the reply) selects those choices, read-only, and the picker says "answered" instead of "3 open". "Answer again" opens them (kept for the tab). With every card answered the dock goes, since nothing is left to send; a part-answered sheet keeps the dock for the open cards and leaves the answered ones out of the line. The hosted picker's `open` counts the same way (`build_questionnaire.open_cards`). Known limit: `sent` is the first keyed message after the reply, so a second answer sent after "Answer again" shows only once the page was reloaded with the first one still named.
- **Colour and touch.** Text on Send is `var(--paper)` (white on the dark theme's light blue was about 2:1). A picked chip is a wash with an outline and a tick, so Send is the only solid control. On a phone or a touch screen the chips, the buttons, the picker, "← Reader", "Answer again" and the context summary are 44 px. "Opens after these answers" is sentence case, the "prototype" label is muted rather than error red and is hidden on a phone, and the "Depends on" hint is `--muted` (4.5:1).

## The hosted copy, ship 1 (2026-10-26)

The owner asked for a hosted transcript reader to browse from anywhere, even with the laptop off, and then to build it, starting with ship 1 of the plan (a split into small objects and a shell page, all on the laptop). How it works and what it looks like: README.md, "The hosted copy".

- **One page, two ways of loading.** The shell is `template.html` built in hosted mode (`build_prototype.fill_template`): no payload, plus `hosted.js`, which loads `index.json` and then one conversation, document or fleet week as the view needs it. Every view is the local page's own code, so the two cannot drift apart; the hosted mode only hides what needs the laptop and paints the "As of" line.
- **The local page's parsing, per conversation.** `sync.py` builds each conversation with the same builder steps the local page uses (`thread_session`, `captain_events`, `mark_sheets`, `attach_documents`, `link_turns`, the parsed-log cache), so an object is exactly that conversation's entry in the local payload. A test builds both from the same sessions and compares them; on the owner's reading list (22 conversations, 190 documents, about 3,000 fleet events) they matched except where a session wrote between the two builds.
- **What a run builds again.** The manifest keeps each conversation's logs with their (mtime, size), the version of the rules that built it (the builder's, the answer sheet's and sync.py's), a stamp of the steering inboxes and task-instruction files it shows, its fleet rows and its turns' times and wake-up tasks. A run builds again only the conversations where one of those changed (a review found that a parser fix otherwise never reached the objects), re-reads a document whose file changed, and puts the fleet weeks together again from the fleet records (cheap: `link_turns` now indexes turns by home and time). An object is written only when its bytes change, and its stamp in `index.json` with it.
- **Choices the plan left open, made here.** The output is `out/hosted/` rather than a separate `out-hosted/`, so the existing ignore rule and the reader server's guard for `out/` cover it. The fleet weeks keep only the first line of your messages and the replies to you, which is all a timeline row shows; the whole turn opens from its conversation. The hosted list counts the replies that need you since your last message ("2 since your last message", so it does not read as the page's own "need you" count of every ask), only while a live pane runs the conversation; a closed pane's conversation says "· closed pane" instead. The page opens on the newest conversation with such a reply. A build older than 10 minutes (the planned sync heartbeat) reads as "Laptop asleep or not syncing". The answer sheet came later the same night (the owner: it would block the agent otherwise): `sheet.html` is `questionnaire.html` in the same hosted mode, loading one conversation's questionnaires (`a/<id>.json`, built by the local sheet's own `chain_questionnaires`) when one is picked.

## The sealed copy, ship 2, local only (2026-10-26)

The owner then said "we can do a local review first, just build it": ship 2 of the plan, all on the laptop. How to run and review it: README.md, "The sealed copy". Nothing is hosted; ship 3 waits on the written exemption or the Replit alternative.

What it is: `sync.py --seal` (redact with `redact.py`, seal with `upload.js` and `prototype/seal.js`, prune, heartbeat) into a local folder laid out as the bucket; the same shell opens it after asking for the passphrase. Changes from the plan, and why:

- **No upload code at all.** The plan's ship 2 has an `rclone` upload with a dry-run mode; here the local folder is the only target, so nothing can reach a remote by mistake. Ship 3 adds the R2 target behind the same step.
- **Encryption in Node, through WebCrypto.** Python here has no AES library, and adding one is not this build's call; Node, which the reader's server already needs, has the same WebCrypto the browser uses, so one file (`seal.js`) seals in the sync and opens in the page.
- **Gzip before sealing, and each object's path bound in.** Ciphertext does not compress, so the objects are gzipped first (on the owner's 411 conversations: 44 MB down to 15 MB, the index the phone polls every 30 seconds from 364 KB to 65 KB). The path as additional data stops a host serving one object under another's name. `k/params.json` carries a sealed check word, so a wrong passphrase is told apart before anything else loads.
- **Heartbeat 5 minutes, not 10.** The page calls the laptop asleep after 10 minutes; resealing the index every 5 keeps an idle but awake laptop from flickering to "asleep".
- **A new passphrase needs `--new-passphrase`.** Otherwise a mistyped passphrase would silently seal the whole bucket again and lock out every device that remembers the old key.
- **The LaunchAgent is printed, not installed,** and reads the passphrase from the login keychain at each run, so no file holds it.
- **Synthetic redaction samples.** The plan says tests on real redacted samples; no transcript content goes into fixtures, so every sample secret is made up at run time. On the owner's logs the scan found 133 (130 64-hex tokens, 3 bearer tokens) in 17 objects, counted only.
- Decisions 2 to 6 keep their defaults: records in v1 with a 14-day window and open items always, the derived key remembered on the device with "Forget this device", documents under the home folder as the builder finds them, no Send (Send came after: the next section).

## Send from the hosted copy, local only (2026-10-26)

The owner asked for Send in the hosted copy, so an answer line can reach an agent while the laptop is off ("then need to build it now"): the queue of plan section 8, local only. How it works: README.md, "Send, through a queue". Changes from the plan and what stayed open:

- **The write permission is settled for now, not designed.** The queue write rides the signed-in owner session, and the owner accepted the stolen-session risk for now. At go-live that means whatever lets the page write to the bucket (a presigned upload, or a write-scoped token) is reachable by anyone holding that session, who could then queue prompts for live agents. The drain's own checks (the pane runs that session's Claude, not at a prompt), the 12-hour expiry and one-send-per-id limit it, but do not remove it.
- **The delivery is `api.js`'s own send, not a copy of it.** `create({ sendable })` lets the drain list the conversations of the copy it drains instead of the local reading list, and `sendPrompt` is returned from `create`; every pane check, the clearing of the input box and the single prompt are the local Send's code.
- **A sealed request is sealed by the page.** The reader's server stores `q/<uuid>.json.enc` bytes it cannot open (it checks only the envelope); the path is the additional data, so a request cannot be passed off under another id.
- **The caller says sealed or plain.** A drain that read `k/params.json` to decide would turn a bucket that lost it into a plain copy that takes unsealed requests (a security review caught this); the sync passes `--sealed` for the bucket and `--plain` for `out/hosted/`, and a sealed bucket without its params stops the run.
- **At most once, and no replay.** A "sending" receipt is written before the prompt, and every request that opens and has its shape goes on a record kept on the laptop only (in `out/`, `.drain-handled-v2-<hash of the folder's path>.json`: never in the folder drained, never beside it, so a wipe of the folder does not clear it and two folders never share one) until it could no longer be fresh, refusals included. Deleting a receipt in the bucket, or putting a copied request back, therefore sends nothing. Freshness is judged on the request's own sealed time (and its time in the folder), never the folder's time alone; a request dated over 10 minutes ahead is refused, so the page's clock matters.
- **A refusal is final.** Nothing retries a prompt that may or may not have reached a pane; the receipt says why and the owner sends again. The page gives up on a request the laptop never picked up after 12 hours and says so.
- **Reply, Comment and the batch are back.** With a message bar the "Copy pointer" stand-ins are gone: the hosted page's Reply, "Comment on this", "Message about this file" and batch are the local page's code, and only Send differs (it queues).
- **Also fixed on the way.** `sync.py` and `upload.js` wrote the copy's folders and files with the default modes; they are now 0700 and 0600 like the reader's own (W23 reached them only after the rebase). The printed LaunchAgent carries a PATH with `node`, `python3` and `herdr` (launchd's own holds none). A changed shell page now reaches the sealed bucket at once instead of at the next heartbeat.
- **Not built, and the open items.** No live check against a real Herdr (every test and lab run uses a fake that records its calls; a live check needs its own isolated lab). The bucket the page writes to is still a local folder: the presigned upload or token, and the drain reading a remote bucket, belong to ship 3.
- **A security review of the whole branch** found nothing exploitable now; what it listed as hardening, for ship 3: the shell pages sit beside the sealed objects, so at go-live whoever can write that storage could swap the shell and take the passphrase (serve the shell from an origin that cannot be written with the data); the passphrase has no strength check (an offline guess at a copied bucket costs 600,000 rounds a try, not more); gzip before sealing leaves sizes visible; and the scanner still lets `Authorization: Basic`, `Cookie:`, Azure `AccountKey=` and a secret inside a doubly escaped JSON string through (Stripe, Google, npm and Hugging Face keys were added; the rest stay open). It also found the sync's passphrase inherited by every `herdr` call it makes (a Herdr that starts its server from one would hand it to every pane's shell): the build's and the drain's Herdr calls now run without it.
- **Review round 7 (S8 to S11) and the scanner gaps.** S8: a cut-off key now redacts the rest of its BEGIN line and every following line of key material in the shapes the reviewer found (line breaks turned to spaces, key text on the BEGIN line, `> ` quoting, line numbers, a partial last line with text after it) and an armoured PGP block, as a rule that still matches each line one way (timed to 10 MB). S9: the hosted answer sheet carries the reader's "As of" line, turns to "Laptop asleep or not syncing since" once the build is older than the heartbeat, and its Send hint says so, since a stale sheet shows cards that were answered since. S10: the replay ledger moved from beside the target folder to `out/`, keyed by the folder's resolved path. S11: the drain lock holds its pid and is taken over at once when that process is gone (and after 10 minutes whatever it holds). The scanner now also takes `Authorization: Basic`, cookie headers, Azure `AccountKey`/`SharedAccessKey`/`SharedAccessSignature` and a secret in twice-escaped JSON; what stays open is a secret in a shape no rule names.
- **Review round 8 (S12).** The S8 rule let a long base64 line be matched by its run and again by its tail, so a line the rule rejected (a backslash and a letter after it) retried every shorter run: 20,000 characters took 15 seconds. A base64 run is now always taken whole (a lookahead keeps any shorter run from being tried), and the test holds the reviewer's shapes and 300 seeded random texts of the key's own characters under half a second each, since the S7 test used blanks only.

## Pins, Sync and the folded Follow (2026-10-26)

Three asks from the owner: pin messages to come back to them, a Sync button for a reader that had fallen out of step, and Follow Herdr in the folded header.

- **Pins.** A pin names a message by its turn's id and which part: `<id>:in` is the message, `<id>:reply` its reply. The id is the session log's own record id, now kept on every turn (`uid`, from `turn_id`), so a pin holds across rebuilds, a `/clear` that moves the conversation under a new id, and new turns before it. A turn without a usable record id gets a hash of its time and opening words. A pin holds `{key, conv, at, pinned}` and no text: the list reads the message from the page, so nothing new carries message text.
- **Where they are kept.** The local server writes them to `~/.config/pmos-transcript-reader/pins.json` (`PMOS_READER_PINS` moves it), outside the repo, so a new copy of the repo does not lose them: folder 0700, file 0600, replaced whole through a rename (`pinstore.js`). A file that is not pins is moved aside as `pins.json.unreadable`, never overwritten. The hosted copy and a page that cannot reach the server (a file copy, a server started before this change) keep them in this browser's storage; the local page moves browser pins into the file once the route answers. The state poll carries the file's revision, so a second tab sees a change within 2 seconds.
- **The list.** `Pins · n` in the controls opens a panel: this conversation's pins in reading order, or every conversation's, newest pinned first. - **Sync.** `POST /api/transcript-reader/sync` reads Herdr again at once and runs the same read-only rebuild a changed log starts (session list, each session's live or ended state, the turns of every listed session; one build holds them all and the page swaps in the whole of it, so no session waits to be opened to be current). The page shows "Synced HH:MM:SS" (the last time it heard from the server, updated by every poll) and says "Not syncing since ..." when the server stops answering for 8 seconds; the badge reads "offline". ## Search, slice 1 (2026-10-26)

The owner's reading list holds conversations of up to 1,300 turns, a third of whose text the page folds away or never draws, so the browser's own find cannot reach it. The plan (the scout's report, `reader-search-design`) was plain-text search built into the page over the turns it already holds, and its slice 1 is built here: find in this conversation and across the reading list. How it works and its keys: README.md, "Search". The owner's answers to the plan's questions: Cmd/Ctrl+F opens the reader's search, and a second press hands over to the browser's; "All conversations" is the reading list now (every fleet session is slice 4); the phone's "Search all" download is a later slice (slice 1 says in the hosted copy how many conversations the device has loaded); no model.

- **Where it lives.** `prototype/find.js` holds the matching as plain functions with no DOM (fields per conversation, the query parser, the Who filters, ordering, snippets, and `spans`, which finds the words over a part's text nodes), pasted into the reader page's script through its own slot, inside the hashed script, like `pins.js`; `find.test.js` runs it in a vm sandbox. The search row, list, highlights and the way to a match are in `template.html`. No route, no network, no model; the query never leaves the page, so the hosted copy (sealed or not) searches after the turns are open and a host never sees one.
- **A match opens only what hides it, in place.** Each thing that hides text (a run of folded wake-ups, a folded wake-up, a clipped message or reply, working notes) registers its own opener and closer in `reveals`, under the key `state.open` already uses for it, and its click handler calls the same function. `goTo` and `openAt` call them for a match and for a pin, so `openPin` no longer draws the conversation again either; the conversation is drawn once only when another one has to be shown. What the search opened is remembered by the turn's id (`{conv, uid, kind}`), not its number, so a `/clear` that joins turns above, or a rebuild, cannot lose it; closing folds it back, last opened first, in place when the page still holds it, and keeps your place through the top-level piece of the conversation that stays when what is inside it folds.
- **Matching, as the page draws it.** A part is a message (or the instruction a Firstmate doorbell delivered), a reply or a turn's working notes, named like a pin (`<uid>:<part>`); a wake-up's own notice and a repeated doorbell hold no words. A reply is matched without its markdown markers (a phrase across a bold boundary still matches); a message and working notes are drawn as written, so they are matched as written. That is one step past the plan, which stripped markers everywhere: a message that shows `**` on screen should be findable by it. Folding keeps one character for one character (`İ` and the other letters that lower to two characters stay as they were), so an offset in the folded copy is an offset in the text. The folded copy of a conversation is built on the first search and kept while its turns are the same (a signature of its turn count, the words of its replies and notes, the instructions it delivered and the hosted stamp), so the page's rebuild every few seconds folds only a conversation that changed.
- **Highlights without touching the text.** The CSS Custom Highlight API over ranges found with `spans`, capped at 3,000; the current match is stronger, and selection, "Comment on this" and Copy keep working. Where the API is missing, or the drawn text holds none of the words (a link's address), the current message flashes.
- **The current match.** Typing in "This conversation" goes to the first match at or after the turn being read, else the newest (the page opens at the newest turn); a rebuild or Sync keeps the current one by its key and never scrolls, and is never picked for you in "All conversations" until a row or an arrow is used. The count is messages, as in the plan.
- **Who filters.** Anyone, You, Replies (a reply or working notes: both are the agent's words), Needs you (a reply that does) and Firstmate (its instructions, task instructions included).
- **The header.** While searching, the bar folds as the folded header does (the session name or picker, live badge, Sheet link, Follow, the search button and the chevron), and the tabs, controls and the "Synced" line (at phone width) go. At 390 px it is about 150 px tall with the search row and its two rows of chips, against 799 px unfolded in the plan's measure; with the list open it is about half the screen (the list stops at 58% of the height). The chevron or the session name closes the search while it is open. The page keeps the place being read when the header changes height.

With the search button, the "connecting" badge ran over the row and "read-only" wrapped onto a second line. ## Open it from the phone over Tailscale (2026-10-26)

The owner wanted the reader on their iPhone, and the only other device on their tailnet is that phone. Loopback-only with an SSH tunnel was the alternative; the owner chose a listener on the Mac's Tailscale address, with a passcode, accepting that any device on the tailnet can reach the port. How to use it: README.md, "Open it from the phone".

- **Opt-in, one extra listener.** `serve.sh --tailnet` (or `PMOS_READER_TAILNET=1`) passes `server.js` the address from `tailscale ip -4` and the passcode file. `server.js` binds that one address on the same port, besides the loopback listeners, and only for an address in 100.64.0.0/10 on the command line (never `0.0.0.0`). Without Tailscale, an address, or a passcode file it starts loopback only and says why. The listener is a third `http.Server` that hands requests to the same `api.handle` as `'tailnet'`, so which listener a request came in on is known by construction, not read from its headers.
- **A guard per listener.** `herdr-organizer/guard.js` gained a `hosts` option replacing its loopback names; the tailnet listener's guard accepts only `<address>:<port>` as Host and Origin, with the reader's own token. The default is unchanged, so the organizer and its tests are untouched. A loopback listener never accepts the tailnet Host.
- **Sign-in in front of everything on that listener.** `tailnet.js` runs first: the guard's Host, Origin and Fetch-Metadata checks, then a session. Without one a page request gets the passcode page (401) and everything else 401 JSON, whatever the path, so nothing can be probed. With one, the request goes on to the same routes as on loopback, whose own checks (guard and token) run again, so the cookie adds to those checks and never replaces them. The passcode page is a fixed page, 16 px inputs, with its one script and style allowed by hash in its own CSP and no data from the request in it.
- **Passcode and sessions.** The passcode is typed on a terminal (`passcode.js`, raw mode without echo; a pipe or an argument is refused) and stored as a scrypt hash (N 2^15, 16-byte salt) in an owner-only file, compared in constant time. Sessions are random 256-bit tokens, each kept as one owner-only file named by the token's SHA-256 in `<passcode file>.sessions/` (folder 0700), holding an expiry and a digest of the passcode hash that was current at sign-in, so a new passcode or a removed file ends them. They last 30 days idle (a lookup renews the file at most once an hour; the cookie is renewed on every request), and a restart keeps them (changed 2026-10-27, see "One sign-in for the reader and the organizer"). Wrong passcodes are counted per client address before the hash is checked, so a burst cannot beat the lock, and at most four hashes run at once.
- **Left out.** HTTPS (`tailscale serve`), the Replit copy and any phone layout change.

## One sign-in for the reader and the organizer (2026-10-27)

The Herdr organizer page (`apps/wip-herdr-workspace-organizer.html`, port 8765, `setup/herdr-organizer/phone.js`) now also listens on the Tailscale address, and the owner asked for one passcode and one sign-in: "logged in one app meaning both apps being logged in". Browsers scope a cookie to the address, not the port, so one cookie on `100.x.y.z` already reaches both servers. What had to change is that each server remembered its sessions in memory, so neither could recognise the other's.

- **Chosen: a session file per sign-in, in a folder both servers read.** `createSessions` in `tailnet.js` keeps `<passcode file>.sessions/<sha256 of the token>` (owner-only, holding the expiry and a digest of the passcode hash). Both servers take the passcode file from the same place (`PMOS_READER_PASSCODE_FILE`, else `out/passcode.json`), so they share the folder with no setting of their own. A sign-in on either creates the file, either accepts it, and a sign-out on either deletes it, so it ends in both at once.
- **Why not a signed cookie.** A cookie signed with a key derived from the passcode record would need no shared folder, but it cannot be revoked: a sign-out would only clear the cookie in that browser, and a cookie that leaks stays valid for 30 days. The organizer page can restart sessions, so a sign-out that really signs out matters more than having no files. A signed cookie also needs a rule for renewing a sliding session without storing anything, and the files give that for free.
- **Why one file per session, not one shared file.** Two server processes would otherwise rewrite one file on every renewal and lose each other's updates. Each session is written by whoever signs in, renewed by whoever sees it (at most hourly) and deleted by whoever signs out; a rename makes each write whole.
- **A passcode change still signs every device out of both**, because each file carries a digest of the hash current at sign-in and a lookup under a new hash fails and deletes the file. A removed passcode file signs everyone out too (no record, no session).
- **The cookie is `pmos_session`** (it was `reader_session`; one more sign-in after the upgrade). The page token stays per server and per start: it is never in the session, so the reader's token is not the organizer's.
- **What changes for the reader.** A server restart no longer signs the phone out (the sessions are on disk). The throttle for wrong passcodes is still per server, so an address gets its free tries on each. `serve.sh passcode` stays the one passcode command; `setup/serve.sh` has none.
- **Shared code.** `tailnet.js` now takes a `site` (sign-in paths, landing page, wording) and builds its pages and script per site; the reader's output is byte-identical to before (checked against the old file). `isTailnetAddress` and `defaultPasscodeFile` moved into it, and `setup/tailnet-lib.sh` holds the two shell helpers both `serve.sh` scripts use.

- **Same names, same limits, no embedding.** `build_prototype.named_files` finds `.html` and `.htm` names with the markdown rules (`md_mentions` became `file_mentions`, still linear-time; tool calls' file paths count for short names too) and `resolve_mention` takes the size limit as a parameter (8 MB for HTML), with the same home-and-temp-folder rule. - **The server finds a file only by that list.** `html-open` takes an id, never a path, and checks the file again (listed, still there, `.html`/`.htm`, real path under the home or a temp folder, size). The list is never served.
- **Why the address is the credential.** A sandboxed frame without `allow-same-origin` has an opaque origin, so everything it loads is cross-site: no token header, no `SameSite=Strict` cookie, Fetch-Metadata `cross-site`. Letting it use the token would hand the sketch the API; instead `html-open` (token-guarded) mints a random 128-bit key into the address, kept in memory for an hour of disuse (64 at most), and the viewer route checks the key, GET only, and the guard's Host and Origin (a `cross-site` allowance, new in `guard.js` and used only here, as an option that is off by default). On the tailnet listener `tailnet.js` lets that one GET path through without the session cookie. The key only ever reaches a page that already had the session and the token. A CORS fetch from the frame (`Origin: null`) is refused, which is why fonts and module scripts do not load; the reader never sends an `Access-Control-Allow-*` header, here included.
- **Defence in depth.** The iframe has `sandbox="allow-scripts"`; the response carries `Content-Security-Policy: sandbox allow-scripts; default-src 'none'; ...; connect-src 'none'; frame-src 'none'; form-action 'none'; frame-ancestors 'self'` so it is sandboxed even opened alone in a tab; and the page's own policy gained `frame-src 'self'`. Beside the file only a fixed list of types loads, only below its folder (only the folder itself when that is the home or a temp folder), never a dot file or a link out, after realpath.
- **As of now, cheap.** Each load reads the file from disk. The view is kept across the redraws a new build causes, so the sketch does not restart every few seconds; Reload opens it again.
- **Tried in Chrome (155), not in Safari.** Iframe at 390 px, Desktop width, Full screen, a probe sketch whose script could not read the parent, cookies, storage, call `fetch`, navigate the page or open a window, while its own stylesheet, script and picture loaded. Over the tailnet address the flow (sign-in, `html-open`, the frame's cookie-less request) was run with curl: this Mac's browser is sent through a security proxy that blocks tailnet addresses (README, "Not covered"). The phone itself was not used.
- **Known residual.** A sandboxed frame may still navigate itself, and CSP has no `navigate-to` in current browsers, so a malicious sketch could send its own address (and so its key) to an outside server; that key opens only the files beside the sketch, only for a request that reaches this Mac's loopback or Tailscale address with the right Host, and lapses after an hour unused. A sketch is also served with the files below its folder, not only the ones it names. Both were accepted for a viewer of the owner's own agents' output; the narrower options (a per-view cookie, serving only referenced files) were left out because a frame without an origin cannot send cookies and the page's own references cannot be known before it runs.

## The copy on the phone, for reading with the laptop shut (2026-10-28)

The owner asked to read all conversation content and documents from the phone when the laptop is closed, without Replit: "plan on how we can enable the offline access", then "Yes do it, also the html document viewer". The plan agreed in chat: the phone downloads the sealed copy over Tailscale while the Mac answers, keeps it, and reads it from there; no cloud host. How to use it and what it holds: README.md, "Read it with the laptop shut".

**HTTPS on the tailnet, our own listener.** A phone browser stores a copy offline (service worker, Cache and IndexedDB) and decrypts it (WebCrypto) only on a secure context, so the phone address needs HTTPS, with Tailscale's certificate for the machine's own name.
- *Why not `tailscale serve`.* It would terminate TLS and proxy to loopback, so every tailnet request would arrive from 127.0.0.1 and look local: it would skip the passcode gate (the sign-in is only on the tailnet listener). Not shipped. Instead `server.js` adds a third listener, `https.createServer` on the Tailscale address with the certificate files, whose requests go through the same `tailnet.js` lane (a second instance for its own Host and Origin, `https://<name>:<port>`, and a `Secure` cookie) and then `api.js`: the same passcode, the same shared session files, the same token and guard checks. The plain http listener is untouched and keeps working until the owner switches.
- *The port.* 8443, because macOS lets only root bind below 1024 even on one address (checked: `EACCES` on 443 for the Tailscale address). So the phone address carries the port.
- *The name* comes from `tailscale status --json` (`Self.DNSName`) at each start (`setup/tailnet-lib.sh`), never typed in, so renaming the machine in Tailscale changes the address at the next restart. The certificate is fetched by `serve.sh` with `tailscale cert` into `out/tailnet-cert/` (owner-only, gitignored) and read again by the running server when its files change (hourly check), so a renewal needs no restart. Without HTTPS certificates, a name or a usable certificate the reader starts as before and prints one line of why (certificate missing, for another name, unreadable, expired; port taken; no passcode). The guard (`herdr-organizer/guard.js`) gained a `scheme` option for this (default http); live.js counts a page on `https://*.ts.net` carrying the token as served, the plain tailnet address stays http-only.
- *Left out.* The Herdr organizer page's listener (8765): it has its own server and does not fall out of this shared code (only `tailnet.js`'s lane, which it already uses over http), so it stays plain.

**What the phone keeps, and why it is built this way.**
- *The objects are the page's own business; the worker only opens the shell.* The page keeps each sealed object (the bytes as the Mac wrote them) in IndexedDB with the stamp the index named, and the index and the copy's key parameters too. A load asks the Mac only for an object whose stored stamp differs, so a second "Download everything now" fetches nothing and one changed conversation fetches one object (`offline.test.js`). Only ciphertext is stored: the key is the non-extractable one "Remember on this device" already kept, and the passphrase is never stored or sent. A new passphrase (another salt) drops what was stored under the old one.
- *The Mac gets 8 seconds.* A shut Mac gives no answer, and a browser would wait a minute or more to say so. A request to it is aborted after 8 seconds (the wait for the answer to start, never the download) and counts as "not reachable"; the page then reads its stored copy and says how old it is. While known to be down, later requests do not wait again for 30 seconds, apart from the poll's probe and a Send.
- *The shell page and the worker.* `sw.js` (beside the shell pages, written by sync.py into `out/hosted/` and `out/bucket/`, with a manifest and two icons drawn in Python so no image file is in the repo) answers a navigation to `index.html` or `sheet.html` with the Mac's answer when it comes within 4 seconds, else the page its cache holds. The page itself fills the cache with the shell pages, fetching them with the token it holds: servePage gained that one exception (a shell page, only with the valid token, so whoever asks already holds what it contains).
- *Download everything now* fetches what is missing or changed (four at a time) with a progress bar and, when it ends, the count and the stored size; it also asks the browser to keep the storage (`navigator.storage.persist`). Once used, every newer index brings down only what changed, quietly. Objects the index no longer names are dropped.
- *Forget this device* clears the key, every stored object, held sends, both caches and the worker's registration, and asks for the passphrase again (it warns if sends are still held).

**A Send with the Mac away** is sealed by the page as before and held in an outbox in IndexedDB when the queue route cannot be reached; it is posted under its own id when the Mac answers. Nothing about the queue changed: the server stores sealed bytes it cannot read, `drain.js` checks the pane, writes the receipt and never sends an id twice, and a request older than 12 hours by its own sealed time is refused there; the page drops a held one older than that without posting it. **HTML documents in the copy.** Hosted and sealed builds now list the HTML files replies name (`attach_documents(html=True)`, the local page's own finder, so the same names, locations and 8 MB limit). A deferred script cannot stay deferred once inline, so it runs at the end of the body, in order (found by the end-to-end run: the sketch's own `defer` script ran before its elements existed). The text is redacted like every object, so a script's secret is found; that is why scripts are inlined as text and not as `data:` URIs.

The end-to-end run checks the sketch's origin is `null`, that it cannot read the parent, storage or cookies, that its script, stylesheet and picture work and that its own `fetch` is blocked, online and with the Mac stopped.

**The owner's own sessions.** `sync.py`'s default scope adds Claude Code sessions whose working directory is under `~/workspace` to the fleet homes (a pseudo home "workspace", no records of its own; the picker heading "Your own sessions", `where` shows the folder under it; `--workspace DIR`, `--no-workspace`). ## Fast updates over the phone's link (2026-10-28)

The owner saw new replies take much longer after switching to the machine's address on the phone. Both addresses run the same server code; the cause was the link: the phone reached the Mac through a Tailscale relay (about 100 ms round trip, low throughput), and every new reply in any session made the open page download the whole built page (22.8 MB, sent uncompressed as JSON) and parse it.

- **Compression** (`compress.js`, wired in `server.js` before `api.js` sees a request, so every listener has it and every header, guard and passcode check is untouched). A 200 answer of text or JSON type is brotli (quality 5) or gzip compressed when the request's `Accept-Encoding` allows it; `Content-Length` goes (it described the plain text), `Content-Encoding` and `Vary` are added. Errors, HEAD, pictures and an answer that names an encoding already are left alone.
- **BREACH.** The only response that carries the server token next to agent-written text is the built page opened in a tab, and it is compressed. Accepted: BREACH needs the attacker to put chosen text into the same response and to watch its size, and nothing in a reader response reflects request input (the `name` and `have` of the API are never echoed; errors are fixed strings). The attacker would also have to watch the victim's traffic, which is the WireGuard-encrypted tailnet, and to make the victim's browser load the page cross-site many times: the guard refuses cross-site fetches and a framed page. The token is per server start. The JSON of `page` and `delta` carries the token tag unfilled, never the token.
- **Only what changed** (`delta.js`, `GET /api/transcript-reader/delta?name=prototype.html&have=<generated>`). The server keeps digests of the last six builds it has seen (a page it serves, or the `page` route, notes the build; digests per session, per turn, per fleet event, per document, per small value). The answer holds the order of sessions, for each changed session its fields and only the turns after the ones the page already has (`from`, `count`), the fleet events after the first one that differs, documents added, changed or dropped, and any small value that changed, plus the build's template hash. The page (`mergeDelta` in the template) merges it into its own data and goes on through `applyPayload`, so the reading place, open turns, drafts, kept closed-pane sessions and the picker behave as before. A different template hash still reloads the page. The page falls back to the full page when the server has no digest of its build (a restart, an older server, a page older than six builds), when the route is missing, or when a count in the delta disagrees with what the page holds. A 401 or 403 is handled as for any call (the restarted-server and sign-in reloads).
- **A session identified by its newest id.** A `/clear` gives the conversation a newer id, so it arrives whole once and the old id leaves; a session opened or closed between builds is just an addition or a missing id in the order.
- **Measured** (Chrome 155 at 390 px, "Slow 3G" throttling, a local server on a spare port over a scratch copy of the real payload of this machine, a 3.0 MB built page, the old and the new code each): from the new reply being written to it showing, **70 s before, 7 s after** (about 2 s of that is the page's own poll interval, the rest three round trips at 2 s). What changed, and why that and not something else:

- **Chain search by time** (`log_timeline`, `nearest_anywhere`, `head_cleared_at`). A clear's predecessor is the log written within two minutes before it, so the logs written in that window are found by bisecting a list sorted by write time, with the old tie rule (nearest, then earlier in the list). - **Documents.** The roots are resolved once; the tool-touched paths are looked up by last name in the order the scan found them (`Touched`); a text's mentions and a reply's answer sheet are kept by a digest of the text (`kept_mentions`, `parse_questionnaire`; never the text itself), because they depend on the text alone. Whether a file exists is not kept: a file created after the reply shows at the next build (tested).
- **One interpreter that stays** (`reader.py worker`, `worker.js`). With the above a cold rebuild is about 6 s, which is not a second and a half. What is left is mostly what a build learns about the machine's folders and log heads, so the server keeps one `reader.py` and sends it rebuild, follow, adopt, reopen and close as JSON lines; it runs them as the same commands, in one interpreter (`run_in_process`). A request the worker cannot take (it did not start, it died, it answered with something else) runs in a fresh `reader.py` as before; a request over a minute ends the worker; it is replaced after 500 requests or ten idle minutes (a peak of about 390 MB in a cold first build), and it starts itself again from the new files when `reader.py` or a builder changes (so an update never leaves a build on half old code). Reusing each unchanged conversation's finished entry between builds was left out at first, because every part of an entry (documents on disk, inbox files, the list of panes) can change without its log changing, so it needs a dependency record per entry; it was built later, when the worker's own cost showed ("Worker cost", below).
- **The server watches while a page is open** (`watch` in `api.js`). Watching is on only for a page that polled or waits within 20 s, so with no page open nothing is rebuilt, as before; the folders of the listed logs are watched (`fs.watch` reports an append in about 10 ms on macOS), with a half-second sweep under it for a notice the file system misses, and the pause between rebuilds is a quarter second. A log that stays newer than every build (a clock that ran ahead) is rebuilt for once, not in a loop (`rebuiltFor`); a failed rebuild is still held for 30 s and then tried again.
- **The page is told by a held request, not a stream** (`GET events`, `live.waitForBuilds`). A long wait on a plain request keeps the token header, which `EventSource` cannot send, and the compressed answers that would sit in a buffer; it ends with the stamps the moment a build is published (the stamp files are watched too, so a build made by hand is announced), or after 25 s with the same stamps. A page that is hidden does not wait; one that cannot take the build (a passage is being picked) waits a second before asking again; a server without the route (404) leaves the page to its tick. Refreshes are serialised, and one for a build already taken is skipped, so the tick and the wait do not fetch twice.
- **Kept as they were:** the guard, the passcode lane, the token header, the delta and the full-page fallback (a page the server cannot compare still takes the whole page), the sealed copy, the tool-call work. A quiet reader costs one request in 25 s where the tick cost twelve, so the phone over the relay is no worse off.

## Undoing a send (2026-10-30)

The owner asked to be able to undo a sent message, and to see which states can still be undone: "when the message is not really sent, or it's sent but got into a queue and we can still edit the message". The rule behind every choice: **a message is only called undone when whoever holds it can prove it never reached the agent**, and when it is too late the page says so plainly. The map of every state a message passes through, with the evidence, is the investigation's report (outside the repo); this is what was decided.

- **An Undo window of 5 seconds, in the page.** Send holds the message in the page, the box locks and Send turns into "Undo 5" (the faded message in the conversation also has Undo and Send now). Nothing is written to the draft, the batch, the outbox or the queue, and nothing is asked of the server or a pane, until the window ends; so Undo can say "never sent" with no further proof. A same-session reload inside the window leaves the draft in the box, with nothing sent and nothing lost. A full navigation to another session stores a text-only handoff in this tab and resumes the remaining window, preserving the original session id so delivery cannot move to the session now on screen; a same-session navigation discards it. Photo `File` objects cannot survive a full navigation, so photo sends fall back to the reload-safe draft behavior. The rules are `prototype/undosend.js` (`makeSendHold`), tested apart in `undosend.test.js` with a clock the test moves. The click handler takes the one window before anything else, so a fast double click cannot hold two messages, and a session switched to meanwhile (the picker is in the Documents header too) has Send and Add off with "Send is back when alpha's Undo window ends." instead of a button that does nothing, and only the session holding the message answers to Esc, Enter and the Undo click; Reply, "Comment on this", Add, a batch's Edit and Remove, and Enter are off while the box is locked, because each would change what the held message is. Five seconds, because longer makes every send feel slow and shorter is too short to read "Sent" and change one's mind; Send now skips the wait.
- **Undo on a message waiting in the laptop's queue, decided by the holder.** The queue file is the one place both the drain and the page's Undo can act, and a rename is atomic, so each takes the message by renaming it: the drain to `<id>.json(.enc).claimed` (before it reads it), `unqueue` to an empty tombstone `.cancelled-<id>`. Exactly one rename wins and the loser finds the file gone, so "cancelled" and "sent" cannot both be true. This works on a sealed copy because nothing is opened, only renamed, and it works across processes because the drain is a separate `node` run started by `sync.py`. A claim left by a drain that died is picked up by the next run (the "sending" receipt, written before anything is typed, still tells a run that stopped midway from one that never started).
- **A message held on the phone is only "undone" once the laptop says so.** The phone cannot tell a held message that never left from one whose post reached the laptop with only the answer lost (a case the tests already cover). So Undo posts `unqueue` if it can; if the laptop is not reachable it marks the outbox item `cancel` and keeps the message on screen as "Undoing · waiting for the laptop to confirm" (text not given back), and the next flush posts the cancel instead of the message. The laptop answers "cancelled" (it took the waiting copy out, or never had it and now refuses a late post of the same id with 409 `cancelled`) or "too late" (the drain has it, and its receipt follows). Only "cancelled" returns the text to the box. A message older than 12 hours needs no word to the laptop: no drain sends it.
- **Undo gives the text back, so there is no separate Edit.** Mail's "undo send" brings the draft back; so does this: into the box when it is empty, into the batch when it is not, so nothing typed meanwhile is overwritten. The text is the final text (pointer included), so a reply's pointer chip does not come back as a chip, only as the words it was typed as. A refused message gets Edit, which is the same move, since the receipt proves it never reached a pane.
- **What is not undone, and what the page says.** Once the drain has claimed a message, or the Mac's own page has delivered it to Herdr, there is no Undo and the faded message says "too late to undo". If Herdr reported the agent as working at that moment, it adds that Claude Code may hold the message as a queued message and that ↑ in the pane takes it back (the page learns the agent's state from the server's answer to Send, and from the drain's receipt, which gained an `agent` field).
- **Claude Code's own queue, observed and left alone.** Observed on a throwaway Claude Code (2.1.314, a private tmux, no Herdr): a message typed while the agent runs a tool call is queued, listed above the spinner, and the composer reads "Press up to edit queued messages"; the session log gets `queue-operation enqueue`, then `dequeue` and a `user` record with `promptSource: "queued"` when the turn ends. One Up key moves the message back into the composer (log: `popAll`, no `user` record), Ctrl+U empties it, and the agent never sees it. So withdrawing is possible and provable from the log. It was not built: it means sending keys to a live agent pane; Up takes **every** queued message in that pane, including ones typed there by hand, not just this one; and Up after the turn ended recalls an older message from history, so clearing it looks like success and withdrew nothing. A safe version would read the log first (an `enqueue` of this text with no `dequeue`), send Up and the clear keys, and only report "taken back" after the log shows `popAll`; it would also need a check that the pane's screen is the one Claude Code draws, since the keys can change between versions. That is for the owner to decide. The reader does not read `queue-operation` records today, so a "Claude Code is holding it" badge backed by the log (rather than by the agent's state at send time) would also need a parser change.
- **Left out.** The answer sheet's Send (its own page and send-once rule; it shares `live.js` and could take the same window). The Replit relay: its storage has no atomic rename, so Undo there would be a cancel marker that `push.py` fetches before the messages and the drain honours, with the verdict read from the laptop's receipt; until that exists the page's Undo says the host cannot take a message back and the message is still sent. Cancelling a request already in flight to the pane (a one-to-two-second window that only opens after the Undo window).

## Documents by task, for a leader's conversation (2026-11-01)

A leader (the captain's Firstmate supervisor sessions: `leader-workspaceos`, `leader-esim`, `leader-nip` and the rest) handles many tasks at once, and each task keeps its notes in `data/<task id>/` of the home it runs in (brief, report, boards, pages, pictures). ## Asks that mean what they say (2026-11-01)

Fix group 1 of the design review (`data/reader-sbds-design-review/report.md` findings C1 to C3, plan in `data/reader-task-tags-design/report.md` section 4.1). Every reply that asked was yellow "Needs you" for good, so in a long leader conversation the header said 360 (leader-esim) and the colour could not tell "waiting on you" from "you answered this days ago".

- **The rule.** `build_prototype.mark_asks` gives each reply that needs you an `ask_state`, recomputed from the turns on every build (never kept in the parsed-log cache): **open** while none of your messages came after it, **quiet** once you wrote something later that did not answer it, **settled** once you answered it. `verdict` is unchanged, so the word rules, the search and the answer sheet read what they read before.
- **Settled by a Reply pointer.** A message of yours with "Re your reply at HH:MM" (with the day when it is not today, and the quoted opening words) settles the one reply of that minute. Two replies in one minute are told apart by the quoted words; with none, or no match, nothing is settled. A pointer without a day only matches a reply from the day it was sent.
- **Settled by a keyed line.** `A1=accept;A2=inline` (the answer sheet types one) settles the nearest earlier reply that asked that key, and each key goes to one reply: a later round that asks `A1` again takes the answer and the earlier round stays quiet. A key with no value, or the format's own `A1=...`, answers nothing. A key no earlier reply asked settles nothing. A quiet ask is the safe error, a wrong "settled" is not.
- **What the page does.** Open: the yellow bar, the "Needs you" tag, and the header and the `n` jump count only these (`prototype/asks.js`, run by `asks.test.js`). Quiet: a grey "Asked" tag, no bar, no box, no highlight. Settled: a grey "Answered" tag; a reply with an answer sheet says "Answered · open the answer sheet". A copy built before `ask_state` existed reads every ask as open. The hosted list's "since your last message" count and the search's Needs you filter use the same open set.
- **The ask box.** An open ask draws the box only while the reply is clipped (the box would otherwise repeat what the text shows); then the body does not highlight the asks a second time, and "Show full reply" removes the box and the highlight comes back. A reply that opens an answer sheet fills the box with the sheet's questions and their recommended defaults (`sheet_cards`, up to four, "and N more in the answer sheet"), with "Answer these questions →" directly under it; the "Reply with: A1=..." line stays in the text.
- **Left out.** Optional offers ("Say go and I will start") still count as asks (finding C11): they are quiet once you write anything later, which is the fix for most of the noise. A later message that answers an ask without pointing at it and without keys leaves it quiet, never settled. The fleet timeline's "open only" still lists every needs-you reply (that view is off by default). The answer sheet's own "already answered" state, layout, and copy belong to fix groups 2 to 4.
- **Checked.** `test_parser.py` (`AskStateTest`: open, quiet, settled by a pointer or a keyed line, the wrong-round cases, placeholders, a repeated key), `asks.test.js`, `find.test.js`, and the page assertions in `test_reader.py`. A copy of this code on a spare port built from the owner's real reading list (leader-esim, 1,524 turns): the header said "1 need you" (360 asks before: 328 quiet, 31 settled by a pointer, 1 open) and exactly one card is yellow; screenshots at 1280 and 390 px are in the task's data folder. Not tried: Safari, the hosted copy on a phone.
## Tool steps, option a (2026-11-01)

The reader kept only a count of a turn's tool calls ("7 tool steps") and threw the calls away at parse time. The owner chose option a for them on 1 Nov (the bearings board's G1, and in the task-tags session): summaries and commands in the phone copy, each failed step's last 400 characters with the turn, tool output only in a per-conversation object (for the last 7 days, or when the owner presses "Download this conversation's outputs"), and the text of a `Read` never. The plan is `data/reader-task-tags-design/report.md` sections 4.2 and 6 (slices 5 to 8); the research behind it is `data/reader-research-tool-calls/report.md` sections 6 and 8. The changes card (slice 9) waits on an open question and is not built.

- **A step is a small record, made at parse time** (`prototype/tool_steps.py`, with short keys; the module header lists them). `build_prototype.session_exchanges` makes one for each `tool_use` and fills it from the `tool_result` that follows (found by `tool_use_id`, so a result that arrives after a queued message started another turn still finds its step). It reads `message.content` only: `toolUseResult` is a second copy of the output and is never looked at. `tool_steps.py` is part of `PARSE_RULES`, so every log is parsed again once.
- **What the row says.** The agent's own description of a command, else the command's first line, a path, a pattern, a URL or a question; at most 140 characters. The grouping is by runs of consecutive calls, and a step carries how many working notes came before it (`o`), so the notes the page already showed in a separate bucket come back between the runs, where Claude's own transcript view keeps them.
- **Edits as diffs.** From the call's own input, with `difflib` and two lines of context around each change, `+3 -1` on the row, at most 1,500 characters (a write's diff is its first lines, its count the whole file's). A diff needs no result text.
- **Nothing a person did not write or an agent did not say.** The text of a file the agent read is never kept (23% of result bytes in the research, and the likeliest place for a secret). A call whose path is a dotfile (`.env`, `.npmrc`, `state/.lock-session`), under a folder that holds credentials (`.ssh`, `.aws`, `.config` ...) or named like a credential, and a shell command that names one, keep the row and the failure marker and nothing of their text. A first version took every word that started with a dot for a dotfile (`f.read()`, `jq .items`, `s/.*run//`) and withheld 21% of the steps on the owner's logs; a second took only a short list of well-known ones, which a review rightly called fail-open (`cat .mysecrets` would have kept its output). A bare word now counts when it starts with a dot and a letter, stands alone (not after a word, a bracket, a slash or a glob star, and not a call or an index) and is not a bare file extension (`.md`), except in the part of a command that is a jq or yq filter, where `.items` is a path into the data and only a well-known dotfile still counts. 11% of the steps are withheld on the owner's logs, mostly dot folders (`.git`, `.agents`, `.claude`) and CSS selectors a script quotes; their rows stay, and the cost is their output, not their summary.
- **Where the text goes.** The steps are in the conversation's own object (the turns the page already loads). The output text (a command's last 1,500 characters, anything else's first 1,500) is moved out of the turns (`take_outputs`) into one object, `t/<id>.json` in the hosted and sealed copies and `out/steps/<id>.json` beside the local page, which a laptop route reads (`GET /api/transcript-reader/steps?conv=`); the page never holds it. A `t/` object is sealed and redacted like the others (`redact.py` already walks every string of an object), and `upload.js`, `push.py`, the Replit app's names and the reader server learned the new folder.
- **The 7 days are the build's.** `index.json` carries `steps_from`, the build's time less 7 days. A step made after it has its output fetched when it opens; a conversation last active after it is part of "Download everything now". An older step offers the download of its conversation's object, which the device then keeps (and "Download everything now" no longer prunes). The page never reads the clock for this, because a phone with a wrong clock or a stale index would show output it should not, or offer a download it does not need. `steps_from` moves every build, so it is left out of what decides whether the index is sealed again (as `as_of` and `since` are).
- **Rows and the sheet.** The summary row, the list ("first 10, then N more": a turn can have 76 steps) and the sheet are in `template.html`; the rules they draw (summary text, grouping, rows of a diff, the tail of an output, where an output comes from) are `prototype/steps.js`, with no DOM, tested in a sandbox (`steps.test.js`). The sheet is a bottom sheet below 640 px and a dialog above; its focus stays inside, Esc closes, the arrow keys move between steps. "Ask about this" is Reply with a pointer that names the step. Rows are 44 px at phone width and on touch screens.
- **Search.** A turn's steps are a part of it (`:steps`): a step's summary, its file and the first 100 characters of its command are searched inline, so a branch, port or path that appears only in a command is found. The Tools chip reads that part alone; Failed reads only the failed steps' words. Full commands and output are not searched in the phone copy (about a fifth more to carry, and the output is not on the device unless asked for). A match opens all of the turn's steps and marks the rows that hold the words (a command is not drawn on its row, so a tint on the row says which step it is).
- **What it cost.** Measured on the owner's logs (658 conversations, 46,551 steps): the conversation objects grow from 12.7 to 17.6 MB gzipped (+39%: commands 2.8 MB, diffs 0.65, failed tails 0.11, the rest summaries, files, times and keys), and the `t/` objects are 8.8 MB gzipped in the bucket, 5.8 MB of it for the conversations active in the last 7 days. So "Download everything now" carries about 10.7 MB more and the bucket about 13.7 MB more. The research's "+5%" for option a was summaries alone. A 300-character command cap would save about 1.0 MB.
- **Left out.** The changes card (slice 9), a Raw toggle, a jump to the next failed step, full-text search of commands and output on the phone, output search through the laptop, and anything for a sub-agent beyond its row.
- **Tried.** A copy of this code on a spare port over made-up logs (a conversation with 15 steps, one failure, edits, a write, a dotfile read, a command naming a credentials file, and a second conversation 12 days old): Chrome at 390 px with a touch screen and at 1280 px, the local page and the sealed copy (the passphrase typed, the output fetched when a recent step opened, the offer for an older conversation's output, the same offline with the server stopped, "Try again" with it back, the downloaded output read offline), and search finding a command in the sealed copy with the server stopped. Screenshots are in `data/reader-tool-steps-build/shots/`. **Not tried:** Safari or a real phone, and `e2e/phone-e2e.js` (it needs WebKit).

- **One name.** The answer sheet is "Answer sheet" in the header (at phone width too, without its arrow), under a reply ("Answer sheet →", "Answered · open the answer sheet") and on the sheet page (its picker label, its empty and error texts).

## Photos in a message (2026-11-02)

The owner asked: "for the reader app, add the ability to send images in the message". The agents are mostly Claude Code sessions, which read a picture when the message names a local image file path, and the owner works from the laptop and from an iPhone over the tailnet, so a photo has to get from the phone to a file on the laptop and then into the text. Behaviour is in the README ("Photos in a message"); these are the choices behind it.

- **Upload first, then Send names the files.** One request carrying text and photos would need a multipart parser and a second body limit; two steps reuse the one body reader. `upload` takes one photo as base64 inside the JSON body every route takes, because the guard accepts only `application/json` on a POST and the point was to keep the upload behind exactly the checks Send has, not a copy of them with a looser content type (a third more bytes on the wire, which a tailnet does not notice for a few MB). `send` takes `images: [<ids>]` and the server adds the paths itself, so what is typed into the pane is composed by the one place that knows the paths: the page never sends a path, and an id cannot be one (`^[0-9a-f]{32}\.(png|jpg|gif|webp)$`, then `lstat`: a plain file of the upload folder, no link).
- **Upload at Send, not at pick.** Uploading on pick would show the exact path in the preview, but it would also leave a file on the laptop for every photo a person picked and removed, and it would break the Undo window's promise that nothing has left the page. So the thumbnails are the page's own (a canvas painted from the picked file, which also keeps the page's content policy as it was: no `blob:` or `data:` image address was added), and a faded message says "+ 3 photos" until the laptop answers with the text as it went, which is what the log line is later matched by.
- **Bytes decide, never the name.** PNG (IHDR first, a size), JPEG (a marker a JPEG starts with), GIF (a screen size), WebP (a chunk and a size that fits the file) and HEIC (an `ftyp` box with a HEIC brand) are the only kinds; the extension of the saved file comes from what the bytes are, and the name the page gave only ever appears in a refusal, stripped of control characters. SVG, AVIF, PDF and a page renamed `.png` are refused. There is no decoder here, so a file with a true header and a broken body is saved as it came; nothing opens it but the agent's own reader.
- **HEIC becomes JPEG.** Claude Code reads PNG, JPEG, GIF and WebP, not HEIC, and an iPhone gives HEIC whenever its settings or the picker do not convert it. `sips -s format jpeg` (macOS, the absolute path `/usr/bin/sips` so the server's PATH does not matter) converts it through two short-lived files in the owner-only folder, and only the JPEG is kept. When it fails the photo is refused with that reason rather than saved in a form the agent cannot read. Chrome on a Mac cannot draw a HEIC either, so its tile names the kind; Safari on the phone draws it.
- **Caps.** 12 MB a photo (a 24 MP iPhone JPEG is 5 to 10 MB), 6 photos and 30 MB a message, 1 GB for the folder, files older than 30 days dropped as a new one arrives (the one place the server deletes anything of its own making; a photo an agent may need again is days old, not months). The page checks the same numbers first, and a test holds the two sets equal; the server is the authority, and a body past the limit is stopped before it is read whole.
- **Never served.** The folder sits under `out/`, which `api.js` already claims whole (every spelling of a path into it is the reader's, and `setup/serve.sh` refuses it), and no route lists or opens it. A reply that names one of these files can still be opened by `image-open`, like any picture a reply names, behind the token and from the page's own list.
- **The hosted copy takes none.** Its queue seals the text so the server never sees it, and a queue body is 64 KB; carrying a photo would mean sealing it in the browser and growing the queue by three orders of magnitude, and the laptop would then hold the owner's photos on the hosted side. Photos stay local-only: the button is dimmed and says so in the bar when tapped, pasted into or dropped on.
- **Left out.** Other file types, showing the photos in the conversation (they show as the path the agent was given), a decoder on the laptop that proves a body is whole, shrinking big photos in the browser before upload, and keeping photos across a reload.
- **Open: permission prompts in the agent's pane.** Claude Code asks before it reads a file outside its working folders. For a session whose folder is this repo the files are inside it; for a session elsewhere (a Firstmate worker in its own checkout) the pane may show a permission question for the first read, which the page then reports as the agent waiting. Not tried here: nothing was typed into a real pane. If it matters, the answer is a read permission for `out/uploads/` in those sessions, not a different folder.
- **Tried.** A copy of this code on a spare port against a fake Herdr that records what would be typed, Chrome at 390 px (touch, iPhone user agent) and 1280 px, light and dark: three photos picked through the button (one a HEIC, converted by the real `sips`), Send held and then delivered with the fake Herdr's prompt holding the words and three absolute paths, a non-image named `.png` (the tile turned red, nothing typed), an SVG and a 13 MB file (left out at pick), a pasted picture, a paste with words (words win), a dropped pair, removing a thumbnail, switching sessions, Undo (no file written), and the hosted copy (the button says why). Tests: `api.test.js` and `tailnet.test.js` hold the server's side, `attach.test.js` the page's.

## Links to one message, and addresses followed in place (2026-11-02)

The captain asked for the reminder lists of open items ("L17: land the pool-full help ...") to carry a link to the message that first asked each, to read its context again before deciding. The address could name a conversation (`#s=`) and a document (`&d=`), not a message, and the page read it only when it loaded.

- **The id.** `&m=` carries the turn's own id from the session log (`ex.uid`, already what pins use), at least its first 6 characters. Not a position or a time: a position shifts with every rebuild and with the folded runs, a time is not unique. The address names the turn, and the page shows that turn's reply (its message when it has none), which is the "message" the captain reads. An exact id wins, then the first prefix match; `message_link.py` writes enough characters (12 or more) that no other turn in the conversation starts the same.
- **Going there is the pin's way there.** `goTo` already opens a conversation drawn elsewhere and what hides a turn, in place (a folded run, a folded wake-up, a clipped reply with `clips`); a message address uses it, so a message in the older session of a `/clear` chain, or inside folded wake-ups, needs nothing new. The outline (`is-linked`, 2.8 s) lasts longer than a pin's, because a link is followed from elsewhere. A build landing right after the jump redraws the page, so the outline is put back on the redrawn reply, continuing the same fade, and the reply is put back under the header while the header grows its Synced line (a ResizeObserver, 3 s).
- **The address goes back to `#s=` once there.** Two reasons: a reload must not jump again, and the same link clicked a second time must be a change, which a hash that stays equal to the link is not.
- **A hash naming both `&d=` and `&m=` is not an address.** Two views, one place; nothing writes one, and the old strict form for `#s=` and `&d=` is unchanged (`address.js`, with `address.test.js`).
- **The lookup reads replies only.** The key a reminder points at is asked in a reply, and the owner's `L17=a` answer holds the same text, so the command looks in the agent's replies alone, oldest first across the chain, and writes the session that holds the turn.
- **Followed in place (the owner's R1=land it).** Helm's pins and any link opened over the conversation on screen changed the address and loaded the whole page again (about 19 MB, 1 to 1.5 s). The page now listens for `hashchange` and shows the address as a fresh load would (`followAddress`: the conversation, a document, a message; a closed pane's conversation listed again; the same notices). Not a loop: the page's own writes are `history.replaceState`, which fires nothing, and an address for the conversation already on screen changes nothing. Proved in a browser: a marker on `window` survives each change, and one change fires one `hashchange`.
- **Not done.** A link in a reply still opens in a new tab (every link does), so from there it is a fresh load; opening it over the current tab would lose the place in the reminder.

## The lite page and the glance feed: the foundation for watching several sessions (2026-11-05)

The captain's answers to the multi-session watch calls (scout report `reader-multi-session-view`, section 6): build in steps (M1 hybrid-steps), the reader's foundation first as a task of its own (M2), lite columns (M3, first 4), a lite page keeps its composer with the server's own safety checks (M5), no native reader now (M6). This is the foundation, items 1 to 3 of the report's section 5. Conn's columns and list are built next, against what is written here. Formats and the page's address: README.md, "The lite page and the glance feed".

**Scope changed (2026-11-05), after the captain reviewed the live watch prototype:** "the titles seems not to help much, can utilize the current left panes for surfacing asks only, remove the tiles on the top and for the column, can enable maximum 3-2 columns with full content/documents/answer sheet". So there are **no tiles**: the glance feed became the feed of a list of open asks (it lost what only a tile needed: the gist of your last message and of the last reply, the turn count and the pane's state), the columns are **at most three**, and a column is **the whole of one conversation**: its turns, all of its documents, and its answer sheet, none of which may send the column to the full reader. The first version left the unnamed files of a leader's task folders and the answer sheet out (both sent a column to the full reader); the sections below are as it is now.

- **What was built.** (1) Every page build also writes, beside the page, the index and one object for each conversation of a page that shows one conversation (`out/live/`), so the data of one conversation is a file read, not a slice of the 34 MB page. (2) The reader page has a third mode, `live` (`out/live/index.html`), made by splitting the page's `HOSTED` flag in two: `LAZY` (conversations and documents come one at a time from objects, as in the hosted shell) and `HOSTED` (there is no laptop to ask: send queues, no follow, no sync of its own). The lite page is lazy and not hosted, so it sends straight to the pane, follows Herdr, polls the state every 2 seconds and waits on `events`, exactly as the local page does. (3) `out/glance.json`, one small record for each conversation (its open asks, its last turn, which pane runs it), for a native client that reads a file. (4) The lite page's **answer sheet**, `out/live/sheet.html`, with one object (`out/live/a/<id>.json`) for each conversation whose reply asks for keyed answers.
- **Per-conversation data: the build writes it, no worker request.** The report offered a worker request with a guarded route, or the build writing the hosted object for what changed. The second was chosen. The worker already holds each conversation's finished bytes (`build_cache.Thread`), `out/steps/<id>.json` is the precedent for a per-conversation file written beside the page, the worker's reply channel is 8 KB (a conversation is up to 4.3 MB), a file survives a worker restart and needs no new protocol or route that talks to the worker, and the server's route for it is a file read behind the same guard as the hosted copy's objects. `sync.py` is not run: an object is the conversation's own pieces (`head + turns + leader + documents`), hashed (stamp) only when its leader or documents changed and written only when its stamp is not the one on disk (the stamps in `live/index.json` seed a restarted worker, so it does not write 21 MB again). A conversation's object is exactly its entry in the page's payload (the same bytes) less `pane` and `reopened`, which the index carries, so a Herdr change rewrites the index and the glance and no object. Both build paths write it (`build_cache.write` and `build_prototype.write_page`), and `test_cache.Writing` compares every file of the two byte for byte, these included.
- **Documents, all of them.** A conversation's object lists the files its messages and replies name, without their text: a markdown file is `lazy` (the page already knows it: it opens through `md-open`, as on disk now), an HTML file or a picture keeps its kind and the page shows it through `html-open` and `image-open`. `out/live/md-files.json` is the lite listing `md-open` also reads (`md-files.json`, which the full page's test pins as lazy files only, is unchanged). For a **leader's conversation** (a run in a Firstmate home) the object also lists every file of the home's task folders, named or not: the files the page's By task view lists (`taskDocuments`: a document whose folder is under `<home>/data/`, so one rule in the page and one in the build, `lite_documents`, and the tests check the two against each other), with the tasks' titles and states the leader record already carried. The first version left these out, so a leader's column could not show a task's report without the full reader. They are **in the conversation's own object**, not an object of their own per home: the page needs no new loading step (`hosted.js` merges an object's `documents` as it did), and a conversation's column loads one file. The cost is that a change to a task file rewrites the leader's object (the largest on the owner's data lists 2,474 files and is 5.1 MB, of which about 0.5 MB is that list), and a leader's column then loads it again; only leaders pay it, only when a task folder changed, and the build does not parse anything more (the folder listing is the kept `Tasks` record, built again only when what it read changed). No per-document objects are written: 3,142 documents, 11 MB, are not worth writing for a view that opens a file by hand.
- **Follow is off in the lite page** until turned on, in a setting of its own (`tr-live-follow`), because Conn's columns are pinned to a conversation (`#s=`) and a shared `tr-follow` would let the focus reader's choice move them. Turning it on goes where Herdr is now: found on the way that a page opened with Follow off swallowed the first focus it saw, so the switch did nothing until Herdr moved again; the first focus counts as seen even with Follow off (the local page has the same edge, and now behaves as its switch says).
- **The answer sheet, in the column.** The local sheet (`questionnaire.html`) is a 1 MB page that holds every listed conversation's questionnaires, and a reply's "Answer sheet →" button was a link to it: in a column it would have left for a page that is not the column's conversation. The sheet's page now has the same two questions as the reader page (`LAZY`: loads one conversation's questionnaires at a time, from objects; `HOSTED`: no laptop, so Send queues), and the lite page's sheet is that page in mode `live`: lazy, not hosted, so it sends straight to the pane through the same `send` route and every check the server makes, as the local sheet does, and takes a build where it stands (the cards redrawn only when they changed, never while a box is typed in or a send is under way). Its data are written by the same build as the reader page's, before the stamp: `out/live/a/<id>.json` is `{ questionnaires }` exactly as the hosted copy's `a/<id>.json` (one `chain_questionnaires` call, which a rebuilt conversation makes once, from the parsed logs it already holds), and `index.json` lists each conversation's sheet under `sheets` (`object`, `stamp`, the picker's `list`) with the sheet's own `sheet_template`. It shows **only the conversation its address names** (`#q=<session>:<time>` from a reply's button, `#s=<id>` from the title bar), so a column's sheet is that conversation's and its picker holds that conversation's questionnaires; "← Conversation" returns to `index.html#s=<id>`. **Not an overlay in the page:** a frame inside the column would need the page's refusal of frames (`frame-ancestors 'none'`, `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, a navigation-only fetch check) relaxed for one page, and the guard is what keeps another page from reading the token; the sheet therefore opens in the same window, as the next page of the column. A column that goes to the sheet and back reloads the lite page (0.8 to 1.2 s, no 34 MB page) and keeps its place as any reload does.
- **The glance feed's rules** are the page's: open asks are `ask_state == "open"` (the header's "need you"); the ask lines are the questions of the reply's answer sheet when it opens one (`sheet_cards`, which is what the page's ask box lists), else the reply's own `ask_lines`; `ask.uid` is the asking turn's id, so a list can open the conversation on it (`#s=<id>&m=<uid>`). It is written only when its content changes, so a client can watch its modification time. It holds no pane state (`status` is as of the build and Conn has Herdr's own), so a Herdr state change no longer rewrites it. What it lost with the tiles: `message`, `reply`, `turns` and `pane.status`; a conversation's record is about half the size (16 KB for the owner's 31).
- **Not changed, and how that was checked.** The full reader and the hosted and phone copies: `HOSTED` keeps every meaning it had except loading, and every existing test passes (JS 593, Python 519; the page-shell tests now also run on the lite page, and `test_sync` reads the two flags where it read `HOSTED`). New: `lite.test.js` (the routes, with no 34 MB page at all; the flags; Follow; what a lazy page loads), `test_live.py` (the files, what a rebuild leaves alone, the glance), more cases in `hosted.test.js`.
- **Checked in a browser** (Chrome 155, a lab copy over made-up logs, a fake Herdr that records every call; nothing real was sent): the lite page opens on `#s=<id>` and loads index and one object only (no page, no delta in the network log); a reply written to the log is on screen in about 1.8 s with the events wait answered, `index.json` and that one object fetched; a send went through the 5-second window and then 25 clear-key calls and one `agent prompt` to the fake pane (`PROMPT w1:p1 A1=approve the three refunds`), with the page saying "Sent ... too late to undo here"; Follow on moved the page to the pane Herdr had focused and loaded that conversation, and a later focus change moved it again; the picker, Pins and a markdown document (through `md-open`) work; no console error.

## Worker cost

Measured 2026-11-02, after the Helm reviewer found `reader.py worker` at 6.7% of a core over 60 s and 18.6% over 300 s with no app open (121 and 235 MB), mostly rebuilding the page as session logs changed. The design study had assumed 0.6%.

- **What cost.** On the owner's 75 listed sessions (35 conversations, a 30.7 MB page) every rebuild did all the work again for a change to one conversation: about 2 s of CPU with nothing changed (1.6 to 2.8 s across runs), 4.5 s with one busy session whose chain holds 550 turns. Per rebuild: 1.0 s resolving and reading the files replies name and walking each leader's task folders (65,000 file system calls), 0.4 s loading each log's parsed turns, 0.4 s writing the page, 0.3 s on the chains of the 75 sessions (the search for a `/clear`'s log looked at every log written since, for each chain), 0.2 s on the answer sheet, 0.2 s on the closed-pane check, nine Herdr commands, and 44.9 MB written (the page, every conversation's tool output again, the sheet). The page is the same size however little changed, and the server started the next rebuild a quarter second after the last one ended, so a session that wrote every second kept the worker 65% busy and a reply took a median 5 s to show.
- **What changed.** `prototype/build_cache.py` keeps each conversation's finished result between builds and builds the page from them (README, "Speed from a log write to the page"); a result is kept while its logs, its home and every file system answer its build was given still hold, and the answers are recorded as they are asked for (stat, lstat, listings, reads: `Reads`, `recording`). A folder is asked what it is, not what is in it, unless it was listed, because a first version that took a folder's modification time for part of every path below it rebuilt 21 to 35 conversations out of 35 on every build. Herdr is asked once for a rebuild and each clear's log once; the clears and linked logs are found by time (`clear_timeline`, `late_linked`); a page or sheet identical but for its build time is not written again. The server starts rebuilds for changed logs at most 2 s apart (`REBUILD_EVERY_MS`): without that limit a build that is ten times cheaper is started ten times as often, and the worker's share only halved (70% to 37% with 114 builds in 120 s, where the 2 s limit gives 22% with 57 and a median reply in 2.1 s).
- **Same page.** `build_prototype.build` + `write_page` stay as the plain way to make the page, and `test_cache.py` compares what the kept build makes with it byte for byte (the page, its stamp, the three file lists, the tool output) on a synthetic fleet after a new turn, a document edited, created and removed, a steering message, a backlog, a task folder's files (cut short and not), a `/clear`, Herdr's panes, a session joining and leaving the list, and 8 random runs of 30 such changes; with the file checks switched off 18 of 30 of those tests fail. On the owner's real list the two payloads are identical (30,339,867 bytes).
- **Numbers.** The old code and the new on two lab servers, started together on the same session list and read the same logs in the same minute (the owner's real folder, or a copy-on-write copy of it that a script writes turns to). One-core CPU %, mean footprint, 2 s samples:

## A seal that stopped at one conversation (2026-11-02)

Size had nothing to do with it: a 1 MB line passes and a 52-character line with the character fails.

So the route reads the feed and adds only what it cannot hold: which live Herdr pane runs each conversation (the scan the `state` route already makes, kept for 700 ms) and `older`, which depends on the time of the call and would make the feed change every minute if it were in the file. A conversation that was cleared is found under any session of its chain, and a pane that two listed conversations claim keeps its newest ask.

## A search of one conversation stays with that conversation (2026-11-07)

The owner's ask: a search of "this conversation" should be session-scoped (switch to another session and it is gone); only the all-conversations search crosses sessions; and while a session has its own search, all-conversations cannot be on until that session turns its own off.

- **What was wrong.** `look` was one search for the whole page. Opened on a conversation with scope "here", it stayed open and ran again over whichever conversation was shown next (the lab, before the change: the search row still open on the other pane with "2 messages", its words marked there). Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+F and the All chip also turned All on over a conversation's own search.
- **Now.** A search of "here" carries the conversation it was opened in (`look.conv`, matched against every id the conversation has had, so a `/clear` keeps it). `render` calls `followSearchSession`: a search left behind by a switch of conversation is parked in `look.own` under its conversation (its marks come off the page; the turns it opened stay open in their own conversation, as `state.open` already keeps them, and fold back when that search is closed there), and the conversation now on screen shows its own parked search if it has one. Closing a search (✕, Esc, the magnifier) turns it off for good; switching away only parks it. A search of "all" has no owner: it stays on across a switch to a conversation with no search of its own, and gives way to one that has its own (its words are not kept: it is closed, and opened again with the key).
- **Exclusion.** While the conversation on screen has its own search, the All chip carries `aria-disabled` (dimmed, still pressable), and pressing it, or Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+F, says in the search row's note "This conversation has its own search on. Close it first to search all conversations." for 7 seconds and changes nothing. All is available as soon as that search is closed. A search of another conversation, parked, does not block All here: only the conversation on screen is meant by "a session having its own search". From All, the "This conversation" chip (or `/`) starts the on-screen conversation's own search, which turns All off.
- **Kept across a reload.** The tab's saved search (sessionStorage) now holds the open search and the parked ones; a reload keeps each, the one on screen shown and the others waiting in their conversations.
- **Checked** in Chrome against the lab (fake Herdr): before, 8 of 16 checks failed with the behaviour above; after, 16 of 16 (`e2e/search-scope-check.js`), and `searchscope.test.js` runs the state rules on stand-ins.

## A new Herdr tab, built on a pick (2026-11-07)

The owner's ask: a session opened in a new Herdr tab took "quite some time" to reach the reader with Follow on; "enable a feature to select a new Herdr tab and quickly build it for the reader app".

**Where the time went (measured before changing anything).**

- *On the owner's list* (103 listed ids, 38 conversations; a scratch copy of the reading list, the pane records and the parsed logs, built into a scratch folder with the real logs and Herdr read-only; the machine at load average 13, so read the order, not the digit): a rebuild worker started cold takes 10 to 19 s for its first build (29 s under load); a warm one takes 1.1 to 2.6 s with nothing changed, 1.5 to 2.4 s of CPU to add one session (`adopt`, what Follow and Sync run). A profile of that add: 3.3 of 3.9 s went on walking the chain of every listed session (`reader.drop_closed`, the sweep for closed panes, asks `PaneView.members` of each of the 103, and the builders ask again; one walk of a chain costs about 20 ms, 153 of them). The Herdr reads cost 10 ms each, the log lookup for one session and the scan nothing worth a name, and the page swap about a second.
- *End to end in a lab* (65 conversations, a fake Herdr, a headless browser, a new Claude tab added to it while Follow was on and focus moved to it): the session was on screen 3.8 to 8.7 s after the tab appeared. Of that, 1.9 to 3.4 s passed before the server listed it (the page asks about Herdr every 2 s, and a build already running is waited for), 1.2 to 4 s was the build, 0.6 to 1.5 s the page taking it. Follow also only acts on the pane Herdr has focused, so a tab opened while another is focused waits for the focus.
- *So there are three costs:* the wait to learn of the tab (the poll, and focus), the build (mostly chain walking that a new tab does not change), and, after a quiet spell, a worker that has been let go (`IDLE_MS`, 10 minutes) and has to start cold.

**What was built.**

- **The picker offers the tabs.** The state the page polls now carries `tabs`: each live Herdr pane running Claude, Codex or Copilot whose session has a log and is not on the reading list (a tab taken off the list by hand is offered too: choosing it is a new yes). The session picker shows them as a group, **Herdr tabs not on the reader · n** (status, name, "build now"), between the running sessions and Closed panes. Choosing one shows "Building ...", lists it and builds at once, and opens its conversation when the build is in; the group loses it. No Follow, no focus. Computing `tabs` costs the poll almost nothing: a Claude log is a look in the project folders, a Codex rollout is found in the list the scan already made, a Copilot log is a folder named for the session; no sessions folder is walked (`api.test.js` still holds the Codex walk count at one per scan).
- **A quick rebuild.** `POST /api/transcript-reader/add-tab {session_id}` runs `reader.py tab <id>` in the worker: the id is listed as `follow` lists it (and a close by hand is forgotten), then `rebuild(quick=True)`: no sweep for closed panes (the next ordinary rebuild does it) and `chains_frozen(quick)`, which starts from the chains the last whole rebuild in this process worked out and works out only what it lacks; what it finds is not kept, so the next whole rebuild walks every chain again. A chain that changed since (a `/clear` in another pane) reaches the page with the rebuild its log's change starts, as before.
- **A worker that stays warm.** The worker is no longer let go after 10 idle minutes while a page has been seen in the last hour (`keepWarm`; about 200 to 260 MB resident on the owner's list, and it ends once no page has been seen for an hour), so a tab picked after a quiet spell does not start a cold one.

**After.** On the owner's list, CPU to add one session: `adopt` 1.5 to 2.4 s, `tab` 0.44 to 0.47 s (wall: 1.8 to 3.4 s against 0.7 to 0.9 s). In the lab, from picking the tab to its conversation on screen: 0.6 s warm, 2.1 s for the first build on a cold worker (against 3.0 to 8.7 s, the first build of a run included, with Follow); from the tab appearing in Herdr to the picker offering it: 1.0 to 1.9 s (one poll). `e2e/tab-pick-check.js` (6 of 6), `e2e/tab-latency.js` (the measurement), `api.test.js`, `worker.test.js`, `test_reader.py`, `test_resident.py`.

**Not changed.** Follow still works as it did. The ordinary rebuild still walks every chain (1.1 to 2.6 s warm on the owner's list for a rebuild that changed nothing); making that cheaper for every build, by keeping chains between builds, is a separate change with its own risk of a stale join, and was not needed to make a new tab fast.
