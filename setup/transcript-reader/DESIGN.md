# Transcript reader - design checkpoint

Status: phase 1 prototype, 2026-10-17. Decisions answered 2026-10-21 (see the end); the overall shape is still being tried on chosen sessions before the real build.

## What was built

A throwaway prototype in `prototype/`: `build_prototype.py` (Python standard library, no model, no network) reads the Claude Code session logs of the main home and every registered second mate, plus each home's fleet records, and fills `template.html`.
Run `python3 setup/transcript-reader/prototype/build_prototype.py` from the repo root, then open `setup/transcript-reader/out/prototype.html`.
The page embeds private transcript text, so `out/` is gitignored. `--stats` prints counts only.

- **Conversation** (view A): one session read back in order, in a 27rem column (phone width). Wake-ups fold to a one-line label, and three or more in a row collapse to one line. Every other turn gets `Needs you` or `No action` from fixed word rules. A `Needs you` reply shows its asking lines first, highlighted. A reply over 90 words shows its first lines and a "Show full reply" button. Buttons and `j`/`k`/`n` jump between your messages and between asks. A Firstmate instruction shows the steering message it delivered, not the pointer line.
- **Fleet timeline** (view B): one time-ordered list across you, the main firstmate, the second mates and their workers. Each row shows who to whom. Phone width has no room for seven swim lanes. The rows are update lines and steering messages from the fleet records, plus your messages and the homes' replies, which only the transcripts have. A row opens its full text and, when found, the turn that handled it. A "Waiting on you" panel comes from each home's own summary.

## What the real data showed

Corpus: 30 home sessions, 2026-09-17 to 2026-10-17, 3,030 turns. Fleet view: the last 72 hours.

- **Folding.** 82% of turns are wake-ups. Folding the ones that ask nothing hides 72% of turns but only 36% of the words. The 12% of wake-ups that do ask (305, mostly after a worker update) hold another 23% of the words.
- **Runs.** Folded wake-ups come in 422 runs: median 2 turns, one run in ten 12 or more, the longest 143.
- **Messages sent while busy.** 75 of your 360 messages (21%) arrived while the agent was mid-turn. They sit inside the running turn, not at a turn start. A parser that splits only at turn starts misses one message in five. The prototype splits there.
- **Rule reliability**, hand-checked blind (labels written before seeing the rule's verdict). The first 40 replies agreed 78%. The misses were negated asks, reminders of open items and promises about later, and the rules were tuned on them. A fresh 40 then agreed 90%: all 20 wake-ups, 16 of 20 replies to you. The 4 errors were 3 false alarms (optional offers, a bold heading phrased as a question, a bold label) and 1 miss (a polite instruction). The heading, label and instruction cases are fixed. Optional offers still count as asks, which errs toward showing too much.
- **Reminders.** 662 replies only restate items already waiting on you. The prototype marks them `No action` with a small "reminds you of open items" tag.
- **Spine.** In 72 hours: 146 update lines (112 routine), 3 steering messages, 23 of your messages, 32 replies. Your messages and the replies exist only in transcripts. Update lines and steering messages exist only in fleet records, and cleanup deletes them when a task closes. Only 25 of 146 update lines could be tied to the turn that handled them. Routine updates often wake nobody, and some landed while no loaded session was running.
- **Open items.** Folding update keys gives 58 decisions never marked settled. 56 of them are older than three days, and many were settled some other way. The homes' own summaries list 49 decisions waiting on you. 12 of the 19 with a recorded age are over a week old, and 5 of 6 homes flag their books as out of step with live state.

## Decisions for the captain

1. **Wake-ups that ask.** Fold every wake-up, or keep the 12% that ask open? Recommended: keep them open. Folding all would have hidden 305 asks in this month of sessions.
2. **Reminders.** Does "still waiting on you" count as `Needs you`? Recommended: no. Mark it `No action` with the reminder tag, and keep the open list in the fleet view.
3. **How much of a kept reply to show.** Recommended: the asking lines first, then the first lines of the reply, with the rest behind "Show full reply" once it passes about 90 words.
4. **Timeline spine.** Recommended: fleet records plus your messages and the homes' replies, with the handling turn one tap away. Raw turns never become the default rows.
5. **Source of "waiting on you".** Recommended: each home's own summary only. Do not derive open items from update keys.
6. **Scope and delivery.** Recommended: the main home and the second mates, not workers' own sessions. Default window of 3 days, with a session picker for older reads. Rebuild with one command, and serve it with the reader's own server (`setup/transcript-reader/serve.sh`, port 8768) so it opens in a browser tab (see README for the supported browsers). No auto-refresh in the first version.

## Answers (2026-10-21)

1. Keep wake-ups that ask open.
2. Reminders are tagged, not `Needs you`.
3. Ask-first-then-clip is the right layout. How asks get picked out (today: the word rules above) is reopened once the app's overall shape is settled.
4. to 6. As recommended.

To try the shape on particular sessions, keep a reading list with `python3 setup/transcript-reader/reader.py` (`add`, `remove`, `list`, `panes`; no argument rebuilds every listed session). A session can be named by id, by its first 8 characters, or by its Herdr pane label. Only listed sessions are read, each under the home that holds its working folder, and the fleet timeline starts at the earliest of them. ## Answer sheet prototype (2026-10-21)

A grill-me review reply asks for keyed answers (`Reply with: A1=...; A2=...`). `prototype/build_questionnaire.py` finds those replies with fixed rules: a card table with `ID` and `Answer` columns (choices in backticks), a `**A2 revised.**` question followed by backtick choice bullets, a checkpoint table of answers already sent, and `**B1:**` cards under a "locked" heading. `questionnaire.html` lets the choices be picked or written, then copies one line such as `A1=accept;A2=inline`. Only a choice the reply itself calls a recommendation is marked one; none is guessed. The reader links each such reply to the sheet. Three layouts were tried (all cards, one at a time, compact); the captain picked all cards, so only that one remains.

Viewing beside a Herdr tab in VS Code: `workbench.browser.newTabPlacement: "sideGroup"` opens VS Code's Integrated Browser (1.142) in a reused, locked group beside the terminal editor. One key can show and hide that group without closing the page or asking for an address: while a browser tab exists (`browserEditorOpen`), it focuses the first group (Herdr) and toggles it maximized, which hides the browser group and keeps its page; with no browser tab it opens the reader. `runCommands` and `workbench.action.browser.open` go in `terminal.integrated.commandsToSkipShell` so the key works while the Herdr terminal has focus.

```json
{ "key": "ctrl+alt+r", "command": "runCommands", "when": "browserEditorOpen",
  "args": { "commands": ["workbench.action.focusFirstEditorGroup", "workbench.action.toggleMaximizeEditorGroup"] } },
{ "key": "ctrl+alt+r", "command": "workbench.action.browser.open", "when": "!browserEditorOpen",
  "args": "http://localhost:8768/setup/transcript-reader/out/prototype.html" }
```

## Send and follow (2026-10-21)

Like Lavish's "comment and send back to the agent", without an agent blocking on a poll: the served page sends a reply or a passage comment straight into the session's Herdr pane (`herdr agent prompt`, no model involved), and follows the pane Herdr has focused. The captain chose:

1. A focused session that is not on the reading list is added automatically.
2. Send goes straight to the pane, with the exact text visible before sending. No confirm step. (For one comment, "Sends exactly" shows it; for a batch, see below.)

The message points at the reply instead of copying it (its time and first words, plus a quote of at most 100 characters for a passage), because the session already holds the text. Sending, following and refreshing need the page served over http by the reader's own server (`setup/transcript-reader/serve.sh`), so `reader.py` now prints the served links first; the server rebuilds the pages when a listed log changes, which replaces the `file://` auto-reload. The pages under `out/` are now served only through the guarded routes in `api.js`, so the shared static handler's wildcard CORS header no longer exposes them. How it works and what it touches: `README.md`.

**Batches and the exact text (owner's decision, 2026-10-26).** Several comments can be collected and sent as one numbered message, with what is in the box last. That message is longer than the box and the pointer line, so it is not shown in full by default: "Exact text" shows it on a click. To keep the preview from going unnoticed, the bar shows a one-line note while the batch is not empty ("3 comments - Exact text shows what Send types"). No confirm step is added, and Send's routing is unchanged. In "Sends exactly", a pointer longer than 90 characters (a reply the session no longer holds adds a log path and part of the reply) shows as one line that opens on a tap, followed by the words as written; the text sent is the same. This matches README.md.

Not verified end to end: no message was sent to a real pane while building this (the tests fake Herdr), and the VS Code shortcut above was not tried in the captain's window.

## Documents, code boxes and cleared sessions (2026-10-21)

The captain asked for three things while trying the prototype:

1. **Documents a reply names**, such as the two markdown files in the eSIM worker's Oct 14 18:25 review reply, rendered for quick browsing beside their contents list, with comments and messages to the agent. The builder finds markdown file names in a reply by fixed rules (`build_prototype.named_documents`) and embeds each existing file once. The Documents tab shows one file at a time beside its headings (a folding list at phone width), and a selected passage or the whole file can be sent to the session as `Re the file <path>, section "<heading>", the part "<passage>": <comment>`.
2. **Code boxes with a Copy button**, as in the Oct 21 13:46 reply that gave `cd` commands. Code wraps instead of scrolling sideways, so a whole command is visible. Tables in replies and documents now render as tables.
3. **Sessions from before `/clear`**. A cleared pane starts a new log, so the earlier turns had disappeared from the reader. A log whose first prompt is `/clear` continues the log last written within two minutes before it (the clear itself writes that log's final records): the one naming the same remote-control link in any project folder, since a session resumed from another directory keeps its old folder while its `/clear` starts the new log under the current one, else the nearest in the same folder. The reader shows such a chain as one conversation under its newest session.

## A more reliable answer sheet (2026-10-21)

The herdr_fix session's Oct 21 13:55 review got no answer sheet: its choices were written `choice: a / b / c` without backticks. Rather than add one more special case, the card reader now works in layers. Every keyed table row, bold question lead or asked-about keyed bullet is a card. Its choices come from the first rule that finds some: backtick values, lettered `a)` choices (also inside the question), a labelled or slash-separated list of short choices, an option table under a "G2 explained" heading, or a short recommendation such as `accept`. The reply line's example answer and any answer it allows for every card (`needs explanation`, `defer`) are added. A card with none of these is still shown and answered by writing one. A choice is marked recommended only when the recommendation opens with it or names its letter. On two weeks of real replies that ask for keyed answers, sheets found went from 27 to 52 and cards from 103 to 225 (201 with choices). The 13 replies still without a sheet are reminders of an earlier sheet or use other key styles (`FX=`, `P, S, Y = confirm`).

The sheet also no longer reloads itself after every rebuild, which had closed "What this is about" mid-read. It takes the new data in place, redraws only when the open questionnaire changed, keeps the scroll position, and remembers which "What this is about" sections are open.


## The hosted copy, ship 1 (2026-10-26)

The owner asked for a hosted transcript reader to browse from anywhere, even with the laptop off, and then to build it, starting with ship 1 of the plan (a split into small objects and a shell page, all on the laptop). How it works and what it looks like: README.md, "The hosted copy".

- **One page, two ways of loading.** The shell is `template.html` built in hosted mode (`build_prototype.fill_template`): no payload, plus `hosted.js`, which loads `index.json` and then one conversation, document or fleet week as the view needs it. Every view is the local page's own code, so the two cannot drift apart; the hosted mode only hides what needs the laptop and paints the "As of" line.
- **The local page's parsing, per conversation.** `sync.py` builds each conversation with the same builder steps the local page uses (`thread_session`, `captain_events`, `mark_sheets`, `attach_documents`, `link_turns`, the parsed-log cache), so an object is exactly that conversation's entry in the local payload. A test builds both from the same sessions and compares them; on the owner's reading list (22 conversations, 190 documents, about 3,000 fleet events) they matched except where a session wrote between the two builds.
- **What a run builds again.** The manifest keeps each conversation's logs with their (mtime, size), the version of the rules that built it (the builder's, the answer sheet's and sync.py's), a stamp of the steering inboxes and task-instruction files it shows, its fleet rows and its turns' times and wake-up tasks. A run builds again only the conversations where one of those changed (a review found that a parser fix otherwise never reached the objects), re-reads a document whose file changed, and puts the fleet weeks together again from the fleet records (cheap: `link_turns` now indexes turns by home and time). An object is written only when its bytes change, and its stamp in `index.json` with it.
- **Choices the plan left open, made here.** The output is `out/hosted/` rather than a separate `out-hosted/`, so the existing ignore rule and the reader server's guard for `out/` cover it. The fleet weeks keep only the first line of your messages and the replies to you, which is all a timeline row shows; the whole turn opens from its conversation. The hosted list counts the replies that need you since your last message ("2 since your last message", so it does not read as the page's own "need you" count of every ask), only while a live pane runs the conversation; a closed pane's conversation says "· closed pane" instead. The page opens on the newest conversation with such a reply. A build older than 10 minutes (the planned sync heartbeat) reads as "Laptop asleep or not syncing". The answer sheet came later the same night (the owner: it would block the agent otherwise): `sheet.html` is `questionnaire.html` in the same hosted mode, loading one conversation's questionnaires (`a/<id>.json`, built by the local sheet's own `chain_questionnaires`) when one is picked.

## The sealed copy, ship 2, local only (2026-10-26)

The owner then said "we can do a local review first, just build it": ship 2 of the plan, all on the laptop. How to run and review it: README.md, "The sealed copy". Nothing is hosted; ship 3 waits on the written exemption or the Replit alternative.

What it is: `sync.py --dry-run` (redact with `redact.py`, seal with `upload.js` and `prototype/seal.js`, prune, heartbeat) into a local folder laid out as the bucket; the same shell opens it after asking for the passphrase. Changes from the plan, and why:

- **No upload code at all.** The plan's ship 2 has an `rclone` upload with a dry-run mode; here the local folder is the only target, so nothing can reach a remote by mistake. Ship 3 adds the R2 target behind the same step.
- **Encryption in Node, through WebCrypto.** Python here has no AES library, and adding one is not this build's call; Node, which the reader's server already needs, has the same WebCrypto the browser uses, so one file (`seal.js`) seals in the sync and opens in the page.
- **Gzip before sealing, and each object's path bound in.** Ciphertext does not compress, so the objects are gzipped first (on the owner's 411 conversations: 44 MB down to 15 MB, the index the phone polls every 30 seconds from 364 KB to 65 KB). The path as additional data stops a host serving one object under another's name. `k/params.json` carries a sealed check word, so a wrong passphrase is told apart before anything else loads.
- **Heartbeat 5 minutes, not 10.** The page calls the laptop asleep after 10 minutes; resealing the index every 5 keeps an idle but awake laptop from flickering to "asleep".
- **A new passphrase needs `--new-passphrase`.** Otherwise a mistyped passphrase would silently seal the whole bucket again and lock out every device that remembers the old key.
- **The LaunchAgent is printed, not installed,** and reads the passphrase from the login keychain at each run, so no file holds it.
- **Synthetic redaction samples.** The plan says tests on real redacted samples; no transcript content goes into fixtures, so every sample secret is made up at run time. On the owner's logs the scan found 133 (130 64-hex tokens, 3 bearer tokens) in 17 objects, counted only.
- Decisions 2 to 6 keep their defaults: records in v1 with a 14-day window and open items always, the derived key remembered on the device with "Forget this device", documents under the home folder as the builder finds them, no Send (Send came after: the next section).

## Send from the hosted copy, local only (2026-10-26)

The owner asked for Send in the hosted copy, so an answer line can reach an agent while the laptop is off ("then need to build it now"): the queue of plan section 8, local only. How it works: README.md, "Send, through a queue". Changes from the plan and what stayed open:

- **The write permission is settled for now, not designed.** The queue write rides the signed-in owner session, and the owner accepted the stolen-session risk for now. At go-live that means whatever lets the page write to the bucket (a presigned upload, or a write-scoped token) is reachable by anyone holding that session, who could then queue prompts for live agents. The drain's own checks (the pane runs that session's Claude, not at a prompt), the 12-hour expiry and one-send-per-id limit it, but do not remove it.
- **The delivery is `api.js`'s own send, not a copy of it.** `create({ sendable })` lets the drain list the conversations of the copy it drains instead of the local reading list, and `sendPrompt` is returned from `create`; every pane check, the clearing of the input box and the single prompt are the local Send's code.
- **A sealed request is sealed by the page.** The reader's server stores `q/<uuid>.json.enc` bytes it cannot open (it checks only the envelope); the path is the additional data, so a request cannot be passed off under another id.
- **The caller says sealed or plain.** A drain that read `k/params.json` to decide would turn a bucket that lost it into a plain copy that takes unsealed requests (a security review caught this); the sync passes `--sealed` for the bucket and `--plain` for `out/hosted/`, and a sealed bucket without its params stops the run.
- **At most once, and no replay.** A "sending" receipt is written before the prompt, and every request that opens and has its shape goes on a record kept on the laptop only (in `out/`, `.drain-handled-v2-<hash of the folder's path>.json`: never in the folder drained, never beside it, so a wipe of the folder does not clear it and two folders never share one) until it could no longer be fresh, refusals included. Deleting a receipt in the bucket, or putting a copied request back, therefore sends nothing. Freshness is judged on the request's own sealed time (and its time in the folder), never the folder's time alone; a request dated over 10 minutes ahead is refused, so the page's clock matters.
- **A refusal is final.** Nothing retries a prompt that may or may not have reached a pane; the receipt says why and the owner sends again. The page gives up on a request the laptop never picked up after 12 hours and says so.
- **Reply, Comment and the batch are back.** With a message bar the "Copy pointer" stand-ins are gone: the hosted page's Reply, "Comment on this", "Message about this file" and batch are the local page's code, and only Send differs (it queues).
- **Also fixed on the way.** `sync.py` and `upload.js` wrote the copy's folders and files with the default modes; they are now 0700 and 0600 like the reader's own (W23 reached them only after the rebase). The printed LaunchAgent carries a PATH with `node`, `python3` and `herdr` (launchd's own holds none). A changed shell page now reaches the sealed bucket at once instead of at the next heartbeat.
- **Not built, and the open items.** No live check against a real Herdr (every test and lab run uses a fake that records its calls; a live check needs its own isolated lab). The bucket the page writes to is still a local folder: the presigned upload or token, and the drain reading a remote bucket, belong to ship 3.
- **A security review of the whole branch** found nothing exploitable now; what it listed as hardening, for ship 3: the shell pages sit beside the sealed objects, so at go-live whoever can write that storage could swap the shell and take the passphrase (serve the shell from an origin that cannot be written with the data); the passphrase has no strength check (an offline guess at a copied bucket costs 600,000 rounds a try, not more); gzip before sealing leaves sizes visible; and the scanner still lets `Authorization: Basic`, `Cookie:`, Azure `AccountKey=` and a secret inside a doubly escaped JSON string through (Stripe, Google, npm and Hugging Face keys were added; the rest stay open). It also found the sync's passphrase inherited by every `herdr` call it makes (a Herdr that starts its server from one would hand it to every pane's shell): the build's and the drain's Herdr calls now run without it.
- **Review round 7 (S8 to S11) and the scanner gaps.** S8: a cut-off key now redacts the rest of its BEGIN line and every following line of key material in the shapes the reviewer found (line breaks turned to spaces, key text on the BEGIN line, `> ` quoting, line numbers, a partial last line with text after it) and an armoured PGP block, as a rule that still matches each line one way (timed to 10 MB). S9: the hosted answer sheet carries the reader's "As of" line, turns to "Laptop asleep or not syncing since" once the build is older than the heartbeat, and its Send hint says so, since a stale sheet shows cards that were answered since. S10: the replay ledger moved from beside the target folder to `out/`, keyed by the folder's resolved path. S11: the drain lock holds its pid and is taken over at once when that process is gone (and after 10 minutes whatever it holds). The scanner now also takes `Authorization: Basic`, cookie headers, Azure `AccountKey`/`SharedAccessKey`/`SharedAccessSignature` and a secret in twice-escaped JSON; what stays open is a secret in a shape no rule names.
- **Review round 8 (S12).** The S8 rule let a long base64 line be matched by its run and again by its tail, so a line the rule rejected (a backslash and a letter after it) retried every shorter run: 20,000 characters took 15 seconds. A base64 run is now always taken whole (a lookahead keeps any shorter run from being tried), and the test holds the reviewer's shapes and 300 seeded random texts of the key's own characters under half a second each, since the S7 test used blanks only.


## Live status (2026-10-26)

The owner reported that the reader showed a closed session as live. Reproduced on the owner's own reader, read-only (its built page, Herdr's pane list, `~/.claude/sessions`, the session logs; nothing written but a scratch copy of the pane cache).

- **Symptom.** At 08:32 the page built for the owner's reader (23 conversations) held two conversations, `fc23a9db` (fm-hosted-reader-ship1) and `4fb8ac27` (fm-herdr-contrarian-reviewer), with `pane.live: true`. The header read "Herdr esim · ...", the picker showed "Unknown · <name>" with no "closed pane" tag, and the server's state listed both as running in a pane. Their last turns were 4.7 and 5.4 hours old.
- **Trigger.** Both workers had ended: their logs end with a turn and then the trailing `cost-state` / `last-prompt` records Claude writes on exit (log files last touched 04:43 and 04:44), and `herdr pane process-info` for their panes (`wD5:p2`, `wCZ:p2`) lists `zsh` alone in the foreground. When an agent exits, the pane stays and Herdr drops its `agent`, but for these two panes it kept `agent_session` (state `unknown`).
- **Masking.** Three things hid it. (1) The reader checks a pane's foreground only for panes whose `agent` is `claude` (`herdr_inventory` in the builder, `checkSessions` in `api.js`), so a pane naming no agent was taken at Herdr's word and the kept id counted as a running session. (2) The pane still exists, and a session leaves the reading list only when its pane is gone. (3) The server rebuilds on "a listed session left the live panes" (`notePaneChanges`), which never happened because the id never left them; every other rebuild recomputed the same wrong `live: true`. The page also painted its header from the build, not from the 2-second poll.
- **A session the reader gets right.** `69c0c8ac` (fm-herdr-fix-worker, pane `wCS:p2`) ended in the same minute with the same shell in front, but Herdr cleared its `agent_session`; the page showed "Herdr pane closed". And `b17ba736` (pane `wCG:p2`) runs: `claude --resume b17ba736...` in front, a live record in `~/.claude/sessions` holds its id. The two cases differ only in what Herdr kept, which is why the reader has to check the pane itself.
- **The check that would disprove it.** If the explanation were wrong, those two panes would still run Claude: `process-info` would list a `claude` process, and a live record in `~/.claude/sessions` would hold each id. Run at 08:32: `zsh` alone, and no record holds either id (a read-only script over the reading list, `~/.claude/sessions` and Herdr's pane list). After the fix the same panes read as no session, and the page says "closed pane".
- **Fix, at the cause.** A pane that names no agent but keeps a session id no running Claude holds is asked for its foreground, as a Claude pane already was (`herdr_inventory`, `checkSessions`): a shell in front means no session, a Claude in front keeps Herdr's id or takes that process's session. Where no process records exist at all, Herdr is still taken at its word, as before. Tests: `EndedAgentTest` (test_reader.py) and "a pane whose agent has exited..." (api.test.js) use the shapes above; both fail without the fix. The page now paints "Herdr pane closed" and the picker's "closed pane" tag from the poll (`paneLive`), so a session that ends while the page is open changes within 2 seconds and does not wait for a rebuild.
- **Still true, by design.** A session whose pane still exists but runs no agent stays on the reading list, shown as a closed pane; it leaves only when Herdr no longer lists the pane.
- **Running server.** A reader server keeps the `api.js` it started with. The owner's servers (8767 from `setup/serve.sh`, started Oct 24; and the one on 8768) need `bash setup/transcript-reader/serve.sh restart` to take this, Sync and the pins route.

## Pins, Sync and the folded Follow (2026-10-26)

Three asks from the owner: pin messages to come back to them, a Sync button for a reader that had fallen out of step, and Follow Herdr in the folded header.

- **Pins.** A pin names a message by its turn's id and which part: `<id>:in` is the message, `<id>:reply` its reply. The id is the session log's own record id, now kept on every turn (`uid`, from `turn_id`), so a pin holds across rebuilds, a `/clear` that moves the conversation under a new id, and new turns before it. A turn without a usable record id gets a hash of its time and opening words. A pin holds `{key, conv, at, pinned}` and no text: the list reads the message from the page, so nothing new carries message text.
- **Where they are kept.** The local server writes them to `~/.config/pmos-transcript-reader/pins.json` (`PMOS_READER_PINS` moves it), outside the repo, so a new copy of the repo does not lose them: folder 0700, file 0600, replaced whole through a rename (`pinstore.js`). A file that is not pins is moved aside as `pins.json.unreadable`, never overwritten. The hosted copy and a page that cannot reach the server (a file copy, a server started before this change) keep them in this browser's storage; the local page moves browser pins into the file once the route answers. The state poll carries the file's revision, so a second tab sees a change within 2 seconds.
- **The list.** `Pins · n` in the controls opens a panel: this conversation's pins in reading order, or every conversation's, newest pinned first. A pin opens its conversation, unfolds the wake-up run that hides it, and scrolls it under the header. The hosted copy loads the pinned conversations in the background to show their text.
- **Sync.** `POST /api/transcript-reader/sync` reads Herdr again at once and runs the same read-only rebuild a changed log starts (session list, each session's live or ended state, the turns of every listed session; one build holds them all and the page swaps in the whole of it, so no session waits to be opened to be current). Before the rebuild it lists every live Claude pane's session that is not on the list (`reader.py adopt`, the same path as Follow), see "Sync and the live sessions". It then answers like the poll with the time it finished. The page shows "Synced HH:MM:SS" (the last time it heard from the server, updated by every poll) and says "Not syncing since ..." when the server stops answering for 8 seconds; the badge reads "offline". The hosted copy's Sync reads the newest `index.json` now and says "As of ..." (when the laptop built it) beside "synced ...". A server without the route answers 404; the page then reads what it can and says to restart it.
- **Sync and the live sessions (2026-10-26).** The owner's report was that Sync seems to bring only the focused session up to date. ## Open it from the phone over Tailscale (2026-10-26)

The owner wanted the reader on their iPhone, and the only other device on their tailnet is that phone. Loopback-only with an SSH tunnel was the alternative; the owner chose a listener on the Mac's Tailscale address, with a passcode, accepting that any device on the tailnet can reach the port. How to use it: README.md, "Open it from the phone".

- **Opt-in, one extra listener.** `serve.sh --tailnet` (or `PMOS_READER_TAILNET=1`) passes `server.js` the address from `tailscale ip -4` and the passcode file. `server.js` binds that one address on the same port, besides the loopback listeners, and only for an address in 100.64.0.0/10 on the command line (never `0.0.0.0`). Without Tailscale, an address, or a passcode file it starts loopback only and says why. The listener is a third `http.Server` that hands requests to the same `api.handle` as `'tailnet'`, so which listener a request came in on is known by construction, not read from its headers.
- **A guard per listener.** `herdr-organizer/guard.js` gained a `hosts` option replacing its loopback names; the tailnet listener's guard accepts only `<address>:<port>` as Host and Origin, with the reader's own token. The default is unchanged, so the organizer and its tests are untouched. A loopback listener never accepts the tailnet Host.
- **Sign-in in front of everything on that listener.** `tailnet.js` runs first: the guard's Host, Origin and Fetch-Metadata checks, then a session. Without one a page request gets the passcode page (401) and everything else 401 JSON, whatever the path, so nothing can be probed. With one, the request goes on to the same routes as on loopback, whose own checks (guard and token) run again, so the cookie adds to those checks and never replaces them. The passcode page is a fixed page, 16 px inputs, with its one script and style allowed by hash in its own CSP and no data from the request in it.
- **Passcode and sessions.** The passcode is typed on a terminal (`passcode.js`, raw mode without echo; a pipe or an argument is refused) and stored as a scrypt hash (N 2^15, 16-byte salt) in an owner-only file, compared in constant time. Sessions are random 256-bit tokens, each kept as one owner-only file named by the token's SHA-256 in `<passcode file>.sessions/` (folder 0700), holding an expiry and a digest of the passcode hash that was current at sign-in, so a new passcode or a removed file ends them. They last 30 days idle (a lookup renews the file at most once an hour; the cookie is renewed on every request), and a restart keeps them (changed 2026-10-27, see "One sign-in for the reader and the organizer"). Wrong passcodes are counted per client address before the hash is checked, so a burst cannot beat the lock, and at most four hashes run at once.
- **Left out.** HTTPS (`tailscale serve`), the Replit copy and any phone layout change.

## One sign-in for the reader and the organizer (2026-10-27)

The Herdr organizer page (`apps/wip-herdr-workspace-organizer.html`, port 8765, `setup/herdr-organizer/phone.js`) now also listens on the Tailscale address, and the owner asked for one passcode and one sign-in: "logged in one app meaning both apps being logged in". Browsers scope a cookie to the address, not the port, so one cookie on `100.x.y.z` already reaches both servers. What had to change is that each server remembered its sessions in memory, so neither could recognise the other's.

- **Chosen: a session file per sign-in, in a folder both servers read.** `createSessions` in `tailnet.js` keeps `<passcode file>.sessions/<sha256 of the token>` (owner-only, holding the expiry and a digest of the passcode hash). Both servers take the passcode file from the same place (`PMOS_READER_PASSCODE_FILE`, else `out/passcode.json`), so they share the folder with no setting of their own. A sign-in on either creates the file, either accepts it, and a sign-out on either deletes it, so it ends in both at once.
- **Why not a signed cookie.** A cookie signed with a key derived from the passcode record would need no shared folder, but it cannot be revoked: a sign-out would only clear the cookie in that browser, and a cookie that leaks stays valid for 30 days. The organizer page can restart sessions, so a sign-out that really signs out matters more than having no files. A signed cookie also needs a rule for renewing a sliding session without storing anything, and the files give that for free.
- **Why one file per session, not one shared file.** Two server processes would otherwise rewrite one file on every renewal and lose each other's updates. Each session is written by whoever signs in, renewed by whoever sees it (at most hourly) and deleted by whoever signs out; a rename makes each write whole.
- **A passcode change still signs every device out of both**, because each file carries a digest of the hash current at sign-in and a lookup under a new hash fails and deletes the file. A removed passcode file signs everyone out too (no record, no session).
- **The cookie is `pmos_session`** (it was `reader_session`; one more sign-in after the upgrade). The page token stays per server and per start: it is never in the session, so the reader's token is not the organizer's.
- **What changes for the reader.** A server restart no longer signs the phone out (the sessions are on disk). The throttle for wrong passcodes is still per server, so an address gets its free tries on each. `serve.sh passcode` stays the one passcode command; `setup/serve.sh` has none.
- **Shared code.** `tailnet.js` now takes a `site` (sign-in paths, landing page, wording) and builds its pages and script per site; the reader's output is byte-identical to before (checked against the old file). `isTailnetAddress` and `defaultPasscodeFile` moved into it, and `setup/tailnet-lib.sh` holds the two shell helpers both `serve.sh` scripts use.

## HTML files a reply names (2026-10-26)

Agents leave throwaway sketches and reports as HTML in scratchpad folders and name them in a reply; the Documents view only opened markdown, so from the phone there was no way to see one. Now it does, rendered. How to use it: README.md, "HTML files" under Documents.

- **Same names, same limits, no embedding.** `build_prototype.named_files` finds `.html` and `.htm` names with the markdown rules (`md_mentions` became `file_mentions`, still linear-time; tool calls' file paths count for short names too) and `resolve_mention` takes the size limit as a parameter (8 MB for HTML), with the same home-and-temp-folder rule. An HTML file is a document of kind `html` with no text, so chips, the Documents list, the address (`#d=`) and "Message about this file" work unchanged. `attach_documents(html=True)` is the local page's build only; `sync.py` calls it without, so the hosted and sealed copies are unchanged. `write_page` writes `out/html-files.json` (id to path) with the page.
- **The server finds a file only by that list.** `html-open` takes an id, never a path, and checks the file again (listed, still there, `.html`/`.htm`, real path under the home or a temp folder, size). The list is never served.
- **Why the address is the credential.** A sandboxed frame without `allow-same-origin` has an opaque origin, so everything it loads is cross-site: no token header, no `SameSite=Strict` cookie, Fetch-Metadata `cross-site`. Letting it use the token would hand the sketch the API; instead `html-open` (token-guarded) mints a random 128-bit key into the address, kept in memory for an hour of disuse (64 at most), and the viewer route checks the key, GET only, and the guard's Host and Origin (a `cross-site` allowance, new in `guard.js` and used only here, as an option that is off by default). On the tailnet listener `tailnet.js` lets that one GET path through without the session cookie. The key only ever reaches a page that already had the session and the token. A CORS fetch from the frame (`Origin: null`) is refused, which is why fonts and module scripts do not load; the reader never sends an `Access-Control-Allow-*` header, here included.
- **Defence in depth.** The iframe has `sandbox="allow-scripts"`; the response carries `Content-Security-Policy: sandbox allow-scripts; default-src 'none'; ...; connect-src 'none'; frame-src 'none'; form-action 'none'; frame-ancestors 'self'` so it is sandboxed even opened alone in a tab; and the page's own policy gained `frame-src 'self'`. Beside the file only a fixed list of types loads, only below its folder (only the folder itself when that is the home or a temp folder), never a dot file or a link out, after realpath.
- **As of now, cheap.** Each load reads the file from disk. The view is kept across the redraws a new build causes, so the sketch does not restart every few seconds; Reload opens it again.
- **Tried in Chrome (155), not in Safari.** Iframe at 390 px, Desktop width, Full screen, a probe sketch whose script could not read the parent, cookies, storage, call `fetch`, navigate the page or open a window, while its own stylesheet, script and picture loaded. The phone itself was not used.
- **Known residual.** A sandboxed frame may still navigate itself, and CSP has no `navigate-to` in current browsers, so a malicious sketch could send its own address (and so its key) to an outside server; that key opens only the files beside the sketch, only for a request that reaches this Mac's loopback or Tailscale address with the right Host, and lapses after an hour unused. A sketch is also served with the files below its folder, not only the ones it names. Both were accepted for a viewer of the owner's own agents' output; the narrower options (a per-view cookie, serving only referenced files) were left out because a frame without an origin cannot send cookies and the page's own references cannot be known before it runs.

The click handler takes the send (`sendOnce.begin`) before awaiting anything, so a fast double tap sends once. The rules are `prototype/sendonce.js`, tested apart in `sendonce.test.js`; the record is in memory, so a reload of the page forgets it. Checked at 390 px against a fake Herdr on a spare port: double tap sent one prompt, a failed send re-enabled Send, and an edited answer re-enabled it.

## The copy on the phone, for reading with the laptop shut (2026-10-28)

The owner asked to read all conversation content and documents from the phone when the laptop is closed, without Replit: "plan on how we can enable the offline access", then "Yes do it, also the html document viewer". The plan agreed in chat: the phone downloads the sealed copy over Tailscale while the Mac answers, keeps it, and reads it from there; no cloud host. How to use it and what it holds: README.md, "Read it with the laptop shut".

**HTTPS on the tailnet, our own listener.** A phone browser stores a copy offline (service worker, Cache and IndexedDB) and decrypts it (WebCrypto) only on a secure context, so the phone address needs HTTPS, with Tailscale's certificate for the machine's own name.
- *Why not `tailscale serve`.* It would terminate TLS and proxy to loopback, so every tailnet request would arrive from 127.0.0.1 and look local: it would skip the passcode gate (the sign-in is only on the tailnet listener). Not shipped. Instead `server.js` adds a third listener, `https.createServer` on the Tailscale address with the certificate files, whose requests go through the same `tailnet.js` lane (a second instance for its own Host and Origin, `https://<name>:<port>`, and a `Secure` cookie) and then `api.js`: the same passcode, the same shared session files, the same token and guard checks. The plain http listener is untouched and keeps working until the owner switches.
- *The port.* 8443, because macOS lets only root bind below 1024 even on one address (checked: `EACCES` on 443 for the Tailscale address). So the phone address carries the port.
- *The name* comes from `tailscale status --json` (`Self.DNSName`) at each start (`setup/tailnet-lib.sh`), never typed in, so renaming the machine in Tailscale changes the address at the next restart. The certificate is fetched by `serve.sh` with `tailscale cert` into `out/tailnet-cert/` (owner-only, gitignored) and read again by the running server when its files change (hourly check), so a renewal needs no restart. Without HTTPS certificates, a name or a usable certificate the reader starts as before and prints one line of why (certificate missing, for another name, unreadable, expired; port taken; no passcode). The guard (`herdr-organizer/guard.js`) gained a `scheme` option for this (default http); live.js counts a page on `https://*.ts.net` carrying the token as served, the plain tailnet address stays http-only.
- *Left out.* The Herdr organizer page's listener (8765): it has its own server and does not fall out of this shared code (only `tailnet.js`'s lane, which it already uses over http), so it stays plain.

**What the phone keeps, and why it is built this way.**
- *The objects are the page's own business; the worker only opens the shell.* The page keeps each sealed object (the bytes as the Mac wrote them) in IndexedDB with the stamp the index named, and the index and the copy's key parameters too. A load asks the Mac only for an object whose stored stamp differs, so a second "Download everything now" fetches nothing and one changed conversation fetches one object (`offline.test.js`). Only ciphertext is stored: the key is the non-extractable one "Remember on this device" already kept, and the passphrase is never stored or sent. A new passphrase (another salt) drops what was stored under the old one.
- *The Mac gets 8 seconds.* A shut Mac gives no answer, and a browser would wait a minute or more to say so. A request to it is aborted after 8 seconds (the wait for the answer to start, never the download) and counts as "not reachable"; the page then reads its stored copy and says how old it is. While known to be down, later requests do not wait again for 30 seconds, apart from the poll's probe and a Send.
- *The shell page and the worker.* `sw.js` (beside the shell pages, written by sync.py into `out/hosted/` and `out/bucket/`, with a manifest and two icons drawn in Python so no image file is in the repo) answers a navigation to `index.html` or `sheet.html` with the Mac's answer when it comes within 4 seconds, else the page its cache holds. A refusal or an error opens the stored page; a sign-in page is shown. **Found by running it in WebKit, not by the unit tests:** a worker's own `fetch` of a page carries Fetch-Metadata the reader's server refuses ("Open this page in its own browser tab"), so the worker takes the browser's own navigation answer through navigation preload instead. The page itself fills the cache with the shell pages, fetching them with the token it holds: servePage gained that one exception (a shell page, only with the valid token, so whoever asks already holds what it contains).
- *Download everything now* fetches what is missing or changed (four at a time) with a progress bar and, when it ends, the count and the stored size; it also asks the browser to keep the storage (`navigator.storage.persist`). Once used, every newer index brings down only what changed, quietly. Objects the index no longer names are dropped.
- *Forget this device* clears the key, every stored object, held sends, both caches and the worker's registration, and asks for the passphrase again (it warns if sends are still held).

**A Send with the Mac away** is sealed by the page as before and held in an outbox in IndexedDB when the queue route cannot be reached; it is posted under its own id when the Mac answers. Nothing about the queue changed: the server stores sealed bytes it cannot read, `drain.js` checks the pane, writes the receipt and never sends an id twice, and a request older than 12 hours by its own sealed time is refused there; the page drops a held one older than that without posting it. A post the Mac already has (409) counts as queued, so a lost answer cannot make a second send. `phonequeue.test.js` runs a held Send through the real server and the real drain.

**HTML documents in the copy.** Hosted and sealed builds now list the HTML files replies name (`attach_documents(html=True)`, the local page's own finder, so the same names, locations and 8 MB limit). The viewer's server is not there offline, so `inline_html.py` folds what the file loads from beside it into one text at build time, by the viewer's own rules (only below the file's folder, no dot files, no links out, only the viewer's types, and a flat folder for a file directly in the home or a temp folder), up to 12 MB per document; what cannot be folded stays unloaded and is listed (`skipped`). A deferred script cannot stay deferred once inline, so it runs at the end of the body, in order (found by the end-to-end run: the sketch's own `defer` script ran before its elements existed). The text is redacted like every object, so a script's secret is found; that is why scripts are inlined as text and not as `data:` URIs.
- *Showing it in the same sandbox.* The page cannot give the frame an inline document: a `srcdoc` or `blob:` frame inherits the reader page's own policy (a hash-only `script-src`), which would stop the sketch's scripts. So the page hands the text to the worker over a message channel and the worker answers the frame's navigation with it and the laptop viewer's policy, `sandbox allow-scripts; default-src 'none'; connect-src 'none'; frame-src 'none'; form-action 'none'; ...` (`offline.test.js` compares it to `api.js`'s). **Tried in WebKit before building:** a worker does not answer a frame that carries a `sandbox` attribute (the request reached the network), but it does answer a plain frame, and a `Content-Security-Policy: sandbox allow-scripts` on its response makes the document an opaque origin that cannot read the parent, its storage or its cookies, with scripts running and `fetch` blocked. So the hosted frame has no attribute and the response's policy does the sandboxing. With no worker answering, the address is not served (the server answers 404), so a file never shows unsandboxed. The end-to-end run checks the sketch's origin is `null`, that it cannot read the parent, storage or cookies, that its script, stylesheet and picture work and that its own `fetch` is blocked, online and with the Mac stopped.

**The owner's own sessions.** `sync.py`'s default scope adds Claude Code sessions whose working directory is under `~/workspace` to the fleet homes (a pseudo home "workspace", no records of its own; the picker heading "Your own sessions", `where` shows the folder under it; `--workspace DIR`, `--no-workspace`). A folder that only starts with the same letters (`workspace-old`) is not under it. Every object, these included, goes through `redact.py` as before. Their messages and replies also show in the fleet timeline as from you to "Your own sessions".

**Checked.** The unit suites (`offline.test.js` with a small fake IndexedDB, cache storage and worker over a real sealed bucket, `tailnet.test.js` for the passcode gate and every refusal on the TLS listener with a scratch self-signed certificate, `phonequeue.test.js`, `test_sync.py`, `test_inline_html.py`, `server.test.js` with a fake `tailscale`), and `e2e/phone-e2e.js` in Playwright WebKit with the iPhone profile (34 steps, screenshots at 393 px in the task's data folder). **Not verified:** a real iPhone (Safari's storage rules, the Home Screen app's separate storage, a real shut Mac's timeout), and the real Tailscale certificate (nothing here asked Tailscale for one).
